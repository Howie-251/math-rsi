## 2. Mathematical objects and the essential counting identity

Let \(\mathcal T_N\) be the complete ordered two-color rank-3 signotope
colorings on N vertices satisfying the blue-path-5 and red-path-6 bounds and
containing no weak 7-gon; let \(\mathcal T=\bigcup_N\mathcal T_N\).

The polarity is fixed by the actual recurrence: true triple atoms are red;
`cap` is the blue family and `cup` the red family. Historical clause-group
names with inverted color labels are not the semantic definition. In the
(7,5,6) scope, the forbidden weak 7-gon is a blue cap4 and red cup5 sharing
both outer endpoints, not merely a common right endpoint.

At an endpoint v, take the down-set generated by the exact longest
monochromatic terminal-pair profiles. The path bounds put every such state in
the 3-by-4 box. Its canonical states are exactly

\[
4\ge h_1\ge h_2\ge h_3\ge0,
\]

giving **35 states: 25 ordinary and 10 exceptional**.

The central human-readable lemma is endpoint-state injection. For u<v,
the terminal-pair profile \(\sigma(u,v)\) belongs to \(D(v)\). For any t<u,
the color of (t,u,v) extends one of the two paths ending at (t,u), so
\(\sigma(t,u)\) cannot dominate \(\sigma(u,v)\). Therefore
\(\sigma(u,v)\notin D(u)\), and \(D(u)\ne D(v)\).

Distinctness uses complete coloring and exact path extension; the path bounds
separately ensure membership in the finite catalog. Neither step needs N25,
signotope, or weak-7 exclusion. Injection is a mathematical lemma, **not an
extra all-different clause family silently inserted into the SAT model**.

Define e as the number of occupied exceptional types and m as the number of
globally missing ordinary types. Injection gives

\[
\boxed{N=(25-m)+e=25+e-m.}
\]

In this setting occupied exceptional types correspond to distinct exceptional
endpoints. Arbitrary labels or occurrence slots in a proposed Hall graph still
need their own ownership and non-reuse argument.

An important diagnostic consequence is **N=25 implies m=e already**. Thus an
N25 experiment can discover a structural rule naming missing states, but it
cannot by itself establish a new N>=26 bound. At N25, failure to name a fixed
second missing state is not failure of the numerical existence of a second
missing state.

## 3. The reusable experimental method

### 3.1 Separate discovery from proof

The working loop is:

1. Define the semantic objects, quantified scope, and desired negated claim.
2. Independently decode retained complete SAT models to locate empirical zeros.
3. Establish an audited SAT nonvacuity gate for a conditional transition/context.
4. Try fast external SAT counterexamples first; discard refuted candidates.
5. Escalate persistent candidates to standalone exact CNFs and AE proofs.
6. Require a nonempty bound proof and an independent checker returning zero
   with exactly one `s VERIFIED` verdict.
7. Aggregate only exhaustively accounted-for cells, then independently audit
   the final publication. Keep unknowns and counterexamples explicit.

Empirical absence is candidate-selection data, not a theorem. A raw return
code 20, a timeout, or an operational error receives no UNSAT theorem credit.
Nonvacuity is essential when claiming a meaningful charging edge, but relaxed
SAT in a target-allowed companion does not establish strict nonvacuity.

Complete SAT assignments are checked against every clause and independently
decoded into coloring, paths, targets, endpoint states, occupancy, and counts.
Cached scalars and solver auxiliaries are never the sole semantic authority.

### 3.2 Decompose hard formulas and reuse valid implications

A broad aggregate can remain hard even when a finite partition is tractable.
Use exact q, exact gap, and fixed endpoint pairs with a machine coverage
registry. A verified source CNF can discharge a stronger target by checked
clause inclusion, avoiding a redundant solve. An unresolved weaker source
does not prevent its stronger complementary cells from being proved directly.

### 3.3 Use semantic core extraction for explanation, not as automatic generalization

The triple-only side line used standalone formulas, complete semantic-object
deletions, recursive core extraction, and independently audited deletion
counterexamples. It produced a certified 14,142-clause core with 8,396
semantic objects and 969 variables for one order-19 reference hypothesis.
The 28 hypotheses `Fan(18) and Fan(19) and blue-path [9,a,b,18]`,
10<=a<b<=17, were separately proof-verified in the condensed reference scope.

All seven leave-one-family-out variants of that fixed core were audited SAT.
This only refutes those whole-family deletions from that exact parent; it is
not a MUS, member-level minimality, or global family-necessity theorem. A
complete projection from the original weak-7 encoding was not established.
The line is frozen and is not a dependency of the accepted C1 proof.

### 4.5 Position marks: no free counting capacity, and singleton blockers

Define L_G, I_G, R_G by occurrence before v, strictly between v,w, and after w.
Injection and the exceptional states at v,w give an exclusive disjunction
\(O[G]\leftrightarrow L_G\lor I_G\lor R_G\). Hence

\[
\#\{\text{false atomic region marks}\}=50+m.
\]

The 50 false marks present even when every G occurs are a structural baseline,
not new resources. The count-eligible excess in a G-fibre is all three marks
being false, exactly global missingness of G. Prefix/suffix aliases and
first/last positions do not independently create charging capacity. Marks may
refine an antecedent; charges must still project to distinct base resources.

The nine-model screen gave 135 legal empirical-zero candidates. Prioritized
P0/P1 probes used 23 external SAT calls, all independently audited SAT, with
no proof/checker or Hall calls. Together with retained witnesses they supplied

The accepted conclusion is only
`SINGLETON_CHARGE_OBSTRUCTION_FOR_V1_SINGLE_COORDINATE_FAMILIES`.
It does not exclude Cartesian combinations of these coordinates, set-valued
neighborhoods, or other invariants. At N25 two exceptional endpoints already
imply at least two missing ordinary states via m=e; what failed was naming a
fixed second state uniformly from one of these single context coordinates.

## 5. Why the main line changed to order-generic cardinality

The new question avoids prescribing a named charge before proving its
existence: **can e exceptional types coexist with fewer than the required
number of missing ordinary types?** N25 witnesses guide discovery but are not
used as proof for N>=26.

For 1<=r<=e<=10 define

\[
\mathcal A_{r,e}=
\{X\in\mathcal T_{26+e-r}: e(X)=e,\ m(X)=r-1\}.
\]

The order is forced by the counting identity. There are 55 canonical cells,
not an open-ended enumeration of vertex configurations.

The exact strict CNF is rebuilt at the correct order with bidirectional
state and occupancy definitions, four deterministic at-most encodings for
the two exact counts, and no symmetry breaking. The mathematical bridge is

\[
\Phi^{strict}_{r,e}\text{ SAT}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal A_{r,e}\ne\varnothing.
\]

Both directions are needed. Independent model replay checks the forward
direction; reverse lifting uses the signotope packet path-deletion lemma for
same-outer-endpoint path monotonicity, exact state/occupancy definitions, and
extension completeness of the count encodings. Totalizer auxiliary uniqueness
is not required. Without this reverse implication, CNF UNSAT alone would not
exclude the intended mathematical objects.

For C1, m=0 and e=1,...,10 give exactly N=26,...,35. For later rows, the prior
layer reduces a counterexample to C_r to exact m=r-1. Thus each row needs its
whole e=r,...,10 coverage and the earlier layer hypotheses.
