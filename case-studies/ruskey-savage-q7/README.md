**Every matching of at least 57 edges in the 128-vertex Q7 extends to a Hamilton cycle containing all its prescribed edges.** The earlier Q6 theorem still covers every matching in Q6; imported results also give the at-most-six-direction application in arbitrary dimension. Q7 deepens that same research domain.

A matching with m edges has 128−2m unmatched vertices, or holes. Here μ is the minimum, over the seven coordinate splits, of the absolute difference between the two parity hole counts. Completed μ classes do not represent equal fractions of the remaining problem. The ≥57 theorem retains every edge of the actual matching; it is not obtained by extending a 57-edge subset and assuming other edges survive.

## Reusable mathematical methods

**Control the auxiliary pairing.** A compressed successor can contain edges outside the old P6 input domain. The donor pairing is still a legal choice: choose it so the actual successor has the needed cross-edge interface, use return bridges, then expand the construction and restore the prescribed original edges.

**Count the actual compatible pool.** Source fibers, marked local types and exact compatible completions determine what a finite generation step really covers. A loose count is not a count of compatible original inputs.

**Use one theorem across known legal splits.** A new family theorem may already solve the same matching on another coordinate split or after an allowed monotone completion. Consume these possibilities before dispatching more local work.

**Cover every realization with plans.** A geometry may have one plan valid for every matching realizing it. It may instead have a finite set of plans, with an applicable branch selected from the actual matching. In both cases, coverage and the original-edge-preserving expansion are part of the proof.
