Normalization and scalar uniqueness
Normalized(v) says that some coordinate i is 1 and every earlier coordinate
is zero. It implies v!=0. If a*p=b*q with a,b!=0 and normalized p,q, let i,j
be their first nonzero indices. i<j makes coordinate i read a=0; j<i makes
coordinate j read b=0. Hence i=j and coordinate i gives a=b. Cancellation
coordinate by coordinate gives p=q. Scalar multiples with a!=0 cannot be
zero. The quadratic norm scales by a^2 and cross(p,a*q)=a*cross(p,q), so
nullity and kernel membership survive scaling.
