## 1. Mathematical setting and inherited classification

Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of a hypothetical 59-vertex, 8-regular,
diameter-2 graph, and define the repeat matrix

\[
B=A^2+A-7I-J.
\]

For distinct vertices $u,v$,

\[
B_{uv}=A_{uv}+|N(u)\cap N(v)|-1.
\]

The inherited theoretical analysis gives

\[
B_{uv}\in\{0,1,\ldots,5\},
\qquad
B\mathbf 1=6\mathbf 1.
\]

If $u,v$ belong to different connected components of
$\operatorname{supp}(B)$, then $B_{uv}=0$, so the stronger exact relation

\[
\boxed{A_{uv}+|N(u)\cap N(v)|=1}
\]

holds. Thus a cross-component edge has no common neighbor, while a
cross-component nonedge has exactly one common neighbor.

The inherited quotient analysis reduces the possible component structures
of $\operatorname{supp}(B)$ to four branches:

| Branch | Exact meaning | Status in this document |
|---|---|---|
| `c1` | $\operatorname{supp}(B)$ is connected and nonbipartite | open; not encoded in this run |
| `c3` | the unique three-component quotient type | solver-closed: 343/343 raw UNSAT |
| `c5a` | the first five-cycle quotient type | theoretically closed by a new proof |
| `c5b` | the second five-cycle quotient type | theoretically closed by a new proof |

The exclusions of component counts $7,9,11,13,15,17$ are inherited

\[

## 4. New theoretical exclusion of `c5b`

This branch can be eliminated without its planned 225 SAT cubes.

In the audited cycle order, the relevant blocks are

\[
C_0(6,0)-C_1(6,1)-C_2(12,1)-C_4(20,2)-C_3(15,2)-C_0,
\]

where the second entry is the internal degree. In particular,
$D_1=A[C_1]$ and $D_2=A[C_2]$ are perfect matchings. Let

\[
M=A[C_1,C_2].
\]

The exact cross-component relation gives the block identity

\[
D_1M+MD_2+M=J_{6\times12}.
\]

Choose any matching edge $\{u,u'\}$ of $D_1$ and any matching edge
$\{v,v'\}$ of $D_2$. Let $e$ be the number of ones in the corresponding
$2\times2$ submatrix of $M$. Sum the block identity over these four
positions.

- The $M$ term contributes $e$.
- Left multiplication by $D_1$ exchanges the two selected rows, so
  $D_1M$ contributes $e$.
- Right multiplication by $D_2$ exchanges the two selected columns, so
  $MD_2$ contributes $e$.
- The all-one matrix contributes $4$.

Therefore

\[
3e=4,
\]

which is impossible for an integer $e$. Hence

\[
\boxed{\texttt{c5b}\text{ cannot occur}.}
\]

This argument uses no connectivity assumption and no numerical
approximation. As a computational cross-check, the unsplit zero-cut `c5b`
base CNF (269,184 variables and 971,901 clauses) returned raw UNSAT under
Kissat 4.0.2 in 0.318 seconds. The theoretical proof, not that raw solver
line, is the primary reason to remove `c5b` from the queue.

## 5. New theoretical exclusion of `c5a`

Use the audited cycle order

\[
U(3,0)-V(4,0)-R(20,3)-P(20,1)-W(12,2)-U.
\]

Write $D_W=A[W]$. The $U-W$ incidence partitions $W$ into three
four-point sets $W_0,W_1,W_2$. The exact $U-W$ block equation says that
each $W_i$ is an independent perfect code in the 2-regular graph $D_W$.
Therefore every vertex has one neighbor in each of the other two color
classes. Along every cycle, the colors repeat with period three, so every
cycle length is divisible by 3.

Now choose $p\in P$, let $\bar p$ be its unique neighbor inside the
1-regular graph $A[P]$, and put

\[
S=N_W(p),
\qquad
T=N_W(\bar p).
\]

The quotient degrees give $|S|=|T|=3$. For every $w\in W$, the exact
$P-W$ block equation is

\[
\mathbf1_{w\in S}
+|N_{D_W}(w)\cap S|
+\mathbf1_{w\in T}=1.
\]

Applying the same equation to $\bar p$ shows that:

- $S$ and $T$ are disjoint independent sets;
- there are no edges between $S$ and $T$;
- every vertex outside $S\cup T$ has one neighbor in $S$ and one in
  $T$.

Consequently, every cycle of $D_W$ follows the pattern

\[
S-O-T-O-S-O-T-O-\cdots,
\]

so every cycle length is divisible by 4. Each cycle length is divisible by
both 3 and 4, while $|W|=12$. It follows that

\[
D_W\cong C_{12}.
\]

On this cycle, $S=N_W(p)$ is one of the four residue classes modulo 4,
and $T=N_W(\bar p)$ is the opposite residue class. Each $w\in W$ has
exactly five neighbors in $P$; hence each of the four residue-class types
is used by exactly five vertices of $P$.

Take distinct $p,q\in P$ of the same type. Their matching partners have
the opposite type, so $p$ and $q$ are not adjacent. They nevertheless
share all three of their $W$-neighbors. Thus

\[
B_{pq}
=A_{pq}+|N(p)\cap N(q)|-1
\ge 0+3-1=2.
\]

Every $p$ has four other vertices of its type, giving

\[
\sum_x B_{px}\ge4\cdot2=8,
\]

contrary to $B\mathbf1=6\mathbf1$. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\texttt{c5a}\text{ cannot occur}.}
\]

The contradiction appears before using the $R-P$ equations or classifying
the 20-point 3-regular graph $A[R]$. It explains why adding time to the
generic 264,144-variable `c5a` formula was the wrong next move.
