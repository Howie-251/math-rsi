## 2. Complete rooted reduction

Suppose that a graph \(G\) satisfies

\[
|V(G)|=61,\qquad \Delta(G)\leq 8,\qquad \operatorname{diam}(G)\leq 2.
\]

It is necessarily 8-regular and has 244 edges. Choose a root \(r\) contained
in at most one triangle and write

\[
X=N(r)=\{1,\ldots,8\},\qquad
Y=V(G)\setminus N[r],\qquad |Y|=52.
\]

For \(y\in Y\), define its parent set

\[
P(y)=N(y)\cap X,\qquad p_y=|P(y)|.
\]

If \(t=|E(G[X])|\), the root-defect identity is

\[
2t+\sum_{y\in Y}(p_y-1)=4.
\]

After quotienting by relabelings of \(X\), this gives exactly 60 rooted
templates. Templates are identified structurally by

\[
(t,\ E(G[X]),\ \text{canonical multiset of exceptional parent sets}),
\]

not by matching numeric IDs across independently generated manifests.

### 3.2 Three unconditional CNF strengthening families

All three were generated automatically from each fixed root template.

**U1. Exact local-defect rows for every \(x\in X\).** Let

\[
B_x=\{y\in Y:x\in P(y)\},\qquad
e_x=\sum_{y\in B_x}(p_y-1).
\]

The exact row identity is encoded from the fixed parent structure and the
remaining edges in \(G[Y]\). At the primary-variable level this is a linear
combination of exact degree equations, so its main role is propagation rather
than adding a new mathematical feasibility condition.

For a triangle-free root, a useful equivalent form is

\[
2|E_{G[Y]}(B_x)|+\rho_x=4-e_x,
\]

where

\[
\rho_x=\sum_{y\in Y\setminus B_x}
\bigl(\deg_{G[Y]}(y,B_x)-1\bigr)\geq0.
\]

Thus \(e_x=4\) forces \(B_x\) to be independent and every vertex outside
\(B_x\) to have exactly one neighbor in \(B_x\).

**U2. Weighted neighbor budget for every \(y\in Y\).** Put

\[
q_{yz}=|P(y)\cap P(z)|.
\]

The encoding includes

\[
\sum_{z\ne y}E_{yz}\bigl(q_{yz}+p_z-1\bigr)\leq B_y,
\]

where \(E_{yz}\) denotes adjacency inside \(Y\), and

\[
B_y=
\begin{cases}
5-p_y, & t=0,\\
5-p_y-|P(y)\cap\{1,2\}|, & t=1,
\end{cases}
\]

with the unique fixed \(X\)-edge normalized to \(\{1,2\}\) when \(t=1\).

**U3. Pair common-neighbor caps.** For \(y,z\in Y\), let

\[
h_{yz}=|N_{G[Y]}(y)\cap N_{G[Y]}(z)|.
\]

Then

\[
E_{yz}=1\Longrightarrow q_{yz}+h_{yz}\leq2,
\qquad
E_{yz}=0\Longrightarrow q_{yz}+h_{yz}\leq5.
\]

The implementation uses one shared registry indexed by unordered triples,
with 22,100 triple objects and 66,300 centered roles. No \(c=1\)-conditional
cap was enabled in this iteration.

## 7. Concrete targets for the next mathematical reasoning round

The following patterns are suggested by the solver data, but are not yet
proved lemmas.

### Target A: unify the `[3,3]` family

All four `[3,3]` orbits, corresponding to intersection sizes 3, 2, 1, and 0,
now have raw-UNSAT results. A human proof that a triangle-free root cannot have
profile `[3,3]` would replace four solver observations with one reusable lemma.

### Target B: explain the overlap boundary in `[3,2,2]`

Every `[3,2,2]` template whose three exceptional parent sets have a
disconnected intersection graph has raw-UNSAT evidence. The six unresolved
templates all have connected intersection graphs. A natural candidate lemma is:

> If a triangle-free rooted candidate has exceptional profile `[3,2,2]`, then
> the intersection graph of its three exceptional parent sets must be connected.

This statement still requires proof. The size-three exceptional vertex and its
small weighted-neighbor budget appear to be essential.

### Target C: encode `[2,2,2,2]` as a four-edge multigraph

View the four size-two parent sets as the edges, with multiplicity, of a
four-edge multigraph \(M\) on \(X\). Then

\[
e_x=d_M(x).
\]

The exact-cover behavior of every vertex with \(d_M(x)=4\), together with U2
and U3, may yield a classification lemma for multiple-edge patterns. The two
closed extreme patterns are four equal edges and three equal edges plus an
incident fourth edge; 21 other multigraph types remain open.

### Target D: compare minimally different closed and open templates

The following pairs differ by a small change and are especially useful for
isolating the missing mathematical mechanism:

1. closed `t0_13` versus open `t0_12`;
2. closed `t0_19` versus open `t0_18`;
3. closed `t0_13` versus open `t0_17` (same sorted \(e_x\)-profile);
4. closed `t0_11` versus open `t0_12`;
5. closed `t0_24` versus open `t0_25`;
6. closed `t0_24` versus open `t0_26`;
7. mathematically closed `t1_07` versus open `t1_13`;
8. mathematically closed `t1_05` versus open `t1_06`.

In particular, pair 3 shows that the sorted local-defect row alone cannot be a
complete invariant: both cases have the same sorted row

\[
(3,3,2,1,1,0,0,0),
\]

but different parent-set overlap topology.
