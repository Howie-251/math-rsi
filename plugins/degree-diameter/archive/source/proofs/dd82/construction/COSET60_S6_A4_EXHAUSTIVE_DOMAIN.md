# S6/A4 orbital-union construction: complete finite domain

The two specified S6 actions on 60 vertices each have exactly eight
invariant simple undirected graphs of degree eight. All sixteen unions
were evaluated. Neither action produces a diameter-two graph: its minimum
is 690 unordered bad pairs. A minimizing complete graph in each action
was saved and independently verified to have 60 vertices, 240 edges,
degree eight throughout and diameter four.

The finite computation itself covers the two explicit stabilizers below.
A separate subgroup argument, combined with the completed
[transitive A6 exclusion](COSET60_A6_S3_EXHAUSTIVE_DOMAIN.md), extends the
conclusion to all graphs admitting a transitive S6 action. This is not an
exclusion of all vertex-transitive graphs or all graphs on 60 vertices.

Source: [coset60_s6_a4_search.py](../../../computations/dd82/construction/coset60_s6_a4_search.py).
Data: [coset60_s6_a4_run](../../../computations/dd82/construction/coset60_s6_a4_run/).
The recorded computational scope deliberately remains the two supplied
subgroups; it does not claim a classification or nonisomorphism of actions.

## Explicit construction and exhaustive domain

Let G=S6 on {0,1,2,3,4,5}. Composition p q applies q first. The program
forms all 720 permutations and checks that (01) and (012345) generate
exactly this group.

H1 is generated by (012) and (01)(23), fixing 4 and 5. For H2, label
the six edges of a four-vertex complete graph by

\[
 0=01,\quad1=02,\quad2=03,\quad3=12,\quad4=13,\quad5=23.
\]

The permutations (012) and (01)(23) of its four vertices induce the
six-letter permutations

\[
 [3,0,4,1,5,2],\qquad[0,4,3,2,1,5]
\]

in tuple-of-images notation. These generate H2. The actual closures of
both H1 and H2 contain twelve elements, all even. No classification is
used to generate either subgroup.

Vertices are the 60 left cosets gH, ordered by least unused representative.
The generators of G act by left multiplication. The program checks
that this action is independent of all twelve choices of representative
in every coset. It then computes generator orbits directly on all 1770
unordered vertex pairs. This avoids assumptions about directed orbital
self-pairing or inversion of a coset.

Any invariant simple undirected graph is precisely a union of these
unordered-pair orbits. Both actions have eleven orbitals, with the same
valency multiset

\[
 \{1,1,1,4,4,4,4,8,8,12,12\}.
\]

Every orbital is checked regular on all sixty vertices. Their edge sets
partition K60; the valencies sum to 59. To sum to eight, a subset must
contain either one of the two valency-eight orbitals or two of the four
valency-four orbitals. The three valency-one orbitals cannot contribute
to a further sum of eight. Consequently the complete degree-eight domain
has 2+binomial(4,2)=8 unions per action, agreeing with the independent
subset count and the enumeration iterator.

The search expands all adjacency rows for every union and asserts all
degrees equal eight. By transitivity, if a root has b vertices at distance
greater than two or unreachable, the full unordered bad-pair count is
60b/2=30b. A full single-source BFS therefore suffices for this enumeration.

## Subgroup bridge to all transitive S6 actions

Suppose a graph on 60 vertices admits a transitive S6 action. Its point
stabilizer H has order 720/60=12. If H is not contained in A6, then
A6 H=S6, since A6 has index two. Thus A6 is already transitive on S6/H,
and the completed transitive A6 exclusion applies.

It remains to classify H of order twelve contained in A6. A Sylow
three-subgroup P is cyclic of order three, generated by either a single
three-cycle or a product of two disjoint three-cycles. In both cases

\[
 |N_{A6}(P)|=18.
\]

For a single three-cycle, its normalizer in S6 is S3×S3 on its support
and complement, and its even half has order eighteen. For two
three-cycles, the centralizer in S6 is (C3×C3)⋊C2 of order eighteen;
the block interchange is odd, so its even part is C3×C3 of order nine.
An even product of one inverting transposition on each three-element
support inverts the generator. Since Aut(C3) has order two, the normalizer
in A6 has order eighteen as claimed.

P cannot be normal in H: otherwise H would be a subgroup of this
normalizer, contrary to 12 not dividing 18. Sylow's theorem now gives
exactly four Sylow three-subgroups in H. The conjugation action on these
four subgroups has kernel contained in N_H(P), which has order 12/4=3.
A kernel of order three would equal P and make P normal, a contradiction.
The action is faithful, embedding H as a subgroup of order twelve in S4,
necessarily A4.

Consider a faithful action of A4 on six letters. Its possible orbit sizes
are 1,3,4,6: they divide twelve, and size two would require an index-two
subgroup, impossible since A4 is generated by elements of order three.
If there were only orbits of size one or three, the unique normal V4
would be in the kernel of each orbit action, contradicting faithfulness.
Thus the orbit partition is either 4+1+1 or 6. In the first case the image
is conjugate in S6 to H1. In the second case a point stabilizer in A4 has
order two; all three such subgroups are conjugate in A4. Therefore there
is only one transitive action of degree six up to permutation equivalence,
represented by the action on the six edges of a tetrahedron, namely H2.

This proves that every all-even stabilizer of order twelve is S6-conjugate
to one of the two subgroups actually enumerated. No adjustment to an even
conjugator is required, because the ambient acting group here is S6.
Every corresponding invariant graph therefore lies in one of the two
computed orbital-union domains up to vertex relabelling.

Finally, transitivity makes the graph regular. A 60-vertex graph of
diameter at most two and degree at most seven would violate
60>1+7+7·6=50. Thus a target of maximum degree eight must have degree
eight, exactly the degree searched. Combining the finite sixteen-union
result with the earlier A6 result rules out all such targets admitting
a transitive S6 action.

## Resources and stopping condition

Preparation ran once with a five-CPU-second cap. The complete search ran
once with a thirty-CPU-second cap. Each was pinned to one CPU and used a
1 GiB address-space limit. Both completed without a budget stop; no SAT
solver or extra group enumeration was used.
