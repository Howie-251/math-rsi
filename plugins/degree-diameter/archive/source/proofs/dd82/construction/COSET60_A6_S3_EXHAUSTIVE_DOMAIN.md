# A6/S3 orbital-union construction: complete finite domain

The two specified actions each yield exactly seven simple undirected
8-regular graphs. All fourteen action-labelled unions were evaluated, with
no diameter-two graph. The minimum is 360 unordered bad pairs in either
action; a complete minimizing 60-vertex, 240-edge graph for each action was
saved and independently checked to have diameter three. A subgroup argument
below extends this finite result to every graph on 60 vertices admitting a
transitive A6 action. This does not exclude all vertex-transitive graphs or
all 60-vertex graphs.

Source: [coset60_a6_s3_search.py](../../../computations/dd82/construction/coset60_a6_s3_search.py).
All data are under

## Constructed objects and completeness

Permutations act on {0,1,2,3,4,5}, with product p q meaning p composed with q.
The program generates all 360 even permutations and separately verifies
that the four permutations (0 1 k), k=2,3,4,5, generate precisely this set.
The two stabilizers are

\[
 H_1=\langle(012),(01)(34)\rangle,
 \qquad H_2=\langle(012)(345),(01)(34)\rangle.
\]

Their generated orders are both six. Vertices are the 60 left cosets gH,
ordered by repeatedly choosing the least unused permutation as a
representative. The action is left multiplication. Every generator action
is checked to be a permutation of the cosets and independent of which of
the six representatives of each coset is used.

The generator actions partition all 1770 unordered vertex pairs into
orbits. Thus these are undirected orbitals directly; no assumption about
self-pairing of directed orbitals or inversion of a single coset enters.
Every invariant simple undirected graph is exactly a union of these
orbits. Each orbit is checked regular, with edge count 30 times its
valency. Both actions have ten orbitals with valency multiset

\[
 \{2,3,3,3,6,6,6,6,12,12\}.
\]

The total is 59, accounting for every edge of K60. A valency-eight union
must use the unique valency-two orbital together with either one of the
four valency-six orbitals or two of the three valency-three orbitals.
There are consequently 4+3=7 unions per action. Both the degree subset
counter and the exhaustive subset iterator returned seven.

For each union the program forms the full adjacency matrix and checks
every degree is eight. Transitivity permits single-source BFS: if the root
has b vertices beyond distance two (including unreachable vertices), the
number of unordered bad pairs is 60b/2=30b. No partial radius or proxy
energy replaces this distance test.

## Why these two stabilizers cover transitive A6 actions of degree 60

Let H be any subgroup of A6 of order six. There is no element of order six
in A6: on six letters its possible cycle types would be a six-cycle or
a disjoint three-cycle and transposition, both odd. Hence H is S3 rather
than the cyclic group of order six. Its unique normal subgroup of order
three is generated by a permutation a whose cycle type is either one
three-cycle or two disjoint three-cycles. An involution b outside this
subgroup inverts a.

If a is a single three-cycle, b preserves its support. On that support
b is a transposition; even parity forces its restriction to the remaining
three letters to be a transposition too. These choices are conjugate in
S6 to the generators of H1.

If a has two three-cycle supports, b permutes the two supports. It cannot
exchange them: an involution exchanging two disjoint three-element blocks
is a product of three transpositions and is odd. Thus it preserves both
supports and acts by an inverting transposition on each. Aligning the
two cycles and rotating within their supports gives an S6 conjugate of
H2.

These S6 conjugacies can be chosen even. The odd permutation (34)
centralizes H1. The odd block interchange (03)(14)(25) centralizes H2,
since it exchanges the two equally oriented cycles and the two
transpositions. Composing an odd conjugator with the appropriate odd
centralizer preserves its subgroup image and makes it even. Consequently
every order-six subgroup of A6 is A6-conjugate to H1 or H2.

Any transitive A6 action on 60 vertices has a stabilizer of order
360/60=6 and is permutation-equivalent to left multiplication on its
cosets. Therefore every invariant graph in any such action occurs in
one of the two orbital-union domains, up to relabelling. If a transitive
60-vertex graph had maximum degree at most eight and diameter at most
two, its common degree would be eight: degree at most seven allows at
most 1+7+7·6=50 vertices by the elementary radius-two count. The finite
valency-eight exclusion above therefore rules out all such targets
admitting a transitive A6 action.
