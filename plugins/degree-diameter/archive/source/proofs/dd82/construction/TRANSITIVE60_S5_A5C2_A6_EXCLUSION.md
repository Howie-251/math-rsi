# Three transitive group actions cannot produce an order-60 target

Date: 2026-10-12. A simple graph on sixty vertices with maximum degree
eight and diameter at most two cannot admit a transitive action by
S5, A5 x C2, or A6. This excludes these three acting groups, not every
vertex-transitive graph on sixty vertices and not arbitrary graphs.

## Coset reduction and exact enumerations

A transitive action of G is its action by left multiplication on gH,
where H is a point stabilizer. Every invariant simple undirected graph
is a union of the non-diagonal unordered-pair orbits. The orbital degrees
add, and transitivity makes a root's radius-two ball sufficient to decide
diameter at most two. A target has degree eight: if its constant degree
were at most seven, its radius-two ball would have at most fifty points.

The complete specified-action computations are:

| G | H | Degree-eight orbital unions | Largest closed two-ball |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| S5 | <(01)(23)> | 1281 | 52 |
| A5 x C2 | <((01)(23),0)> | 1999 | 51 |
| A6 | <(012),(01)(34)> | 7 | 48 |
| A6 | <(012)(345),(01)(34)> | 7 | 48 |

All 3294 were checked. Their best complete graphs have respectively
240,270,360,360 bad pairs, as separately confirmed by independent all-source
BFS. Sources, all orbital domains, histograms and actual expanded best
graphs are retained in the reports
[COSET60_S5_C2_EXHAUSTIVE_DOMAIN.md](COSET60_S5_C2_EXHAUSTIVE_DOMAIN.md)
and [COSET60_A6_S3_EXHAUSTIVE_DOMAIN.md](COSET60_A6_S3_EXHAUSTIVE_DOMAIN.md).
Root read both actual generators:
one constructs inverse-paired double cosets, the other directly takes
the generated group orbits on unordered pairs. Each partitions all 1770
unordered pairs, has constant block degrees, and visits exactly the
degree-eight subset count. Neither is a Cayley-generator search.

To cover every transitive action by these groups, it remains to account
for their possible point stabilizers. The following elementary arguments
do so without a transitive-group catalog.

## S5 and A5 x C2: all order-two stabilizers

For both groups, |H|=120/60=2. In S5 an involution is a transposition
or a double transposition. All double transpositions are conjugate, giving
the first enumerated action. If H is generated by a transposition, then
H intersects the normal A5 trivially and S5=A5 H. Hence A5 acts regularly
on the sixty cosets, making every invariant graph an A5 Cayley graph.
These were already excluded by
[CAYLEY_58_61_OBSTRUCTION.md](CAYLEY_58_61_OBSTRUCTION.md).

An involution of A5 x C2 is (1,1), (h,0), or (h,1), where h is a
double transposition. All such h are conjugate in A5. In the first and
third cases, A5 x {0} intersects H trivially and acts regularly, so the
same existing Cayley exclusion applies. The second is precisely the
second enumerated action. This exhausts both groups' stabilizers.

## A6: all order-six stabilizers

Here |H|=360/60=6. A6 has no element of order six: the only order-six
cycle types on six letters are a six-cycle and a disjoint three-cycle
and transposition, both odd. Thus H is S3. Its normal subgroup C3 is
generated either by a single three-cycle or by two disjoint three-cycles.
An involution in H inverts that C3 generator.

For a single three-cycle, its support is preserved. The inverting
involution is a transposition on that support and, to be even, another
transposition on the remaining three letters. The resulting subgroup is
S6-conjugate to H1=<(012),(01)(34)>.

For two three-cycles, an inverting involution cannot exchange their
supports: an involution interchanging two disjoint three-sets consists
of three transpositions and is odd. It therefore preserves each support
and acts there as a transposition, giving an S6-conjugate of
H2=<(012)(345),(01)(34)>.

These S6 conjugacies can always be chosen even. The odd permutation
(34) centralizes H1, and the odd block swap (03)(14)(25) centralizes
H2. If a conjugating permutation is odd, composing it with the appropriate
odd centralizer makes it even without changing the conjugated subgroup.
Consequently every order-six H in A6 is A6-conjugate to one of the two
enumerated stabilizers. The two finite computations cover every transitive
A6 action of degree sixty, even if distinct actions happen to yield
isomorphic individual graphs.

## Limits

This is a local mathematical/finite-computation result. The previous
A5 Cayley exclusion remains an explicit dependency. No classification
of all transitive groups of degree sixty is claimed; a larger group
need not have one of these groups acting transitively. General N(8,2)
remains between 57 and 61, with no graph above 57 found. External review
and Lean formalization of this new result are not complete.
