# Free matching experiment and the doubled HS obstruction

Date: 2026-10-11. No order-60 target graph has been found.

## Complete new object

The earlier C5 search retained three permutations, and subsequent SAT inputs
retained five root matchings. This experiment releases all fifteen matchings
between six fixed Petersen fibers. Thus it preserves 60 vertices, degree 8,
240 edges and within-fiber coverage, but neither restriction above.

`computations/dd82/construction/petersen_all_matchings.cpp` makes ordinary
matching switches, switches guided by an actually missing two-step path, and
occasional coupled switches. One bounded run started from the old 50-bad-pair
candidate. Its objective was the exact number of uncovered unordered pairs.
It used 90.0027 CPU seconds, 90.2312 wall seconds, one CPU, and 156459008
proposals. The best score, 20, first appeared at 8.23993 CPU seconds.
This is not exhaustive search or a minimum claim for the full family.

The best full graph and all missing pairs are saved under
`computations/dd82/construction/petersen_all_matchings_run/` as
`candidate.best.json`, `candidate.best.edges`, and
`candidate.best.verification.json`. Independent all-pairs BFS verifies
60 vertices, 240 distinct edges, all degrees 8, diameter 3, and exactly 20
uncovered pairs. All twenty are between fibers 4 and 5.

## Why this particular family cannot be repaired internally

The analyzer `petersen_double_hs_analysis.py` reads the complete edge list
and checks the following exact structure, without using the search cache.
Deleting fiber 4 or deleting fiber 5 gives a 50-vertex 7-regular graph in
which adjacent vertices have no common neighbor and nonadjacent vertices
have exactly one. Each is therefore a degree-seven Moore graph, with the
Hoffman–Singleton parameters, diameter 2 and girth 5.

There is a bijection from fiber 4 to fiber 5,

    [40,...,49] -> [55,50,51,56,58,57,54,52,59,53],

that preserves their internal Petersen edges and makes corresponding vertices
have identical neighborhoods in the remaining 40-vertex core. Equivalently,
each of the four intermediate-core-fiber routes from u in fiber 4 lands at
the same vertex phi(u) in fiber 5.

For **any** graph retaining this twin attachment structure, and any matching
between fibers 4 and 5, the reachable targets from u in fiber 5 lie among:
one direct target, three routes with the middle vertex in fiber 4, three
with the middle vertex in fiber 5, and the single common core-route target.
There are at most eight distinct targets. Hence at least two targets per
source are uncovered, giving **at least 20 bad pairs** between the two fibers.
The saved graph attains this bound. Its cross-45 route multiplicities are
0 on 20 pairs, 1 on 70 pairs, and 4 on 10 pairs.

This is a sharp obstruction to the twin attachment subfamily only. It does
not exclude arbitrary six-Petersen-fiber graphs or arbitrary order 60.

## Apply the failure to concrete repairs

Changing only one of the four core routes cannot suffice: the other three
still coincide, and the seven direct/internal slots plus at most two core
targets cover at most nine targets. We therefore first released all five
matchings 04,05,14,15,45, changing two core routes simultaneously. The other
ten matchings and all six internal Petersen graphs were fixed.

The complete direct-or-common-neighbor encoding has 5700 variables and
20740 clauses. It returned UNSAT in 0.033701 CPU seconds; the independent
LRAT checker returned exit 20 and `s VERIFIED`. This closes exactly those
five free matchings around this saved candidate.

We then released **all nine** matchings incident to fiber 4 or 5, keeping
only the four-core-fiber induced graph and the six internal Petersen graphs.
This has 14100 variables and 48840 clauses. Its single 30-second run returned
UNKNOWN after 29.884657 CPU seconds. Its partial proof is not a certificate,
and the fixed-core completion remains open. Do not restart that same input.

Both inputs are generated by `petersen_release_five_matchings.py`, using
`--release twin_five` or `--release twin_nine`. Their output directories are
`petersen_release_twin_five_matchings/` and
`petersen_release_twin_nine_matchings/`. All exact-one matching constraints
and every uncovered full-graph pair are encoded; no assumption of twin
attachments is imposed on released edges.
