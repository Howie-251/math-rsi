## 1. Graph and repeat-matrix reduction

Let a simple graph have 63 vertices, maximum degree at most 8, and diameter at most 2. If a vertex has degree at most 7, its radius-two ball contains at most

\[
1+7+7\cdot7=57
\]

vertices. Hence the graph is 8-regular. Write its adjacency matrix as \(A\), and let \(J\) be the all-ones matrix. Set

\[
B=A^2+A-7I-J.
\]

The diagonal entries of \(B\) vanish. Its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative integers: for adjacent vertices they count common neighbors; for nonadjacent vertices they count common neighbors minus one. Every row sum is

\[
64+8-7-63=2.
\]

Thus every connected component of the weighted graph of \(B\) is either a pair of vertices joined by an edge of weight 2, or an ordinary simple cycle of length at least 3. Indeed, a weight-2 edge exhausts both endpoint row sums; in its absence every vertex has two distinct weight-1 neighbors.

Also \(A\) commutes with \(B\): it commutes with \(A^2+A-7I\) and, by regularity, with \(J\).

## 2. Coverage of eigenvalue fields

A cycle of length \(r\le63\) has eigenvalues

\[
2\cos(2\pi j/r).
\]

Reducing \(j/r\) gives \(\mu=\zeta_m^a+\zeta_m^{-a}\), where \(1\le m\le63\), \(\gcd(a,m)=1\). The weight-2 pair has eigenvalues \(2,-2\), already covered by \(m=1,2\). Consequently the norm table must cover all **conductors** \(1\le m\le63\), rather than only cycle lengths that happen to occur.

Let \(C_m\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}\), with \(C_1(X)=X-2\) and \(C_2(X)=X+2\). For \(m\ge3\), the familiar identity

\[
t^{d_m}C_m(t+t^{-1})=\Phi_m(t),\qquad d_m=\varphi(m)/2,
\]

determines it. To justify that the polynomial generated by this identity really is minimal, use irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_m\). Since \(\zeta_m\) is nonreal, while \(\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}\) is real, the extension from the real trace field to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\) has degree exactly 2: it has degree at most 2 by \(t^2-\mu t+1=0\), and cannot have degree 1. Thus the real trace has degree \(d_m\). All primitive choices of \(a\) give conjugate roots and the same norm.

The finite arithmetic lemma is:

\[
N_m=(-4)^{d_m}C_m(-29/4)
\]

is a positive nonsquare integer for every \(1\le m\le63\), except \(m=3\), for which \(C_3=X+1\) and \(N_3=25\). This is exactly the field norm of \(29+4\mu\). If \(29+4\mu\) were a square in \(\mathbb Q(\mu)\), its norm would be a rational square; an integer which is a rational square is an integer square. Therefore the lemma proves that \(29+4\mu\) is a nonsquare in its own eigenvalue field whenever \(\mu\ne-1\).

## 3. The field-of-definition step

This is the main point that should be explicit in the final proof. Real symmetry alone does not force the two quadratic roots to have equal multiplicities.

Let \(W=\mathbf1^\perp\), which is defined over \(\mathbb Q\), has dimension 62, and is invariant under both matrices. On \(W\), \(J=0\). For a real eigenvalue \(\mu\) of \(B\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\mu)\) and

\[
U_{\mu,K}=\{v\in K^{63}: Bv=\mu v,\ \mathbf1^Tv=0\}.
\]

These are linear equations over \(K\), so extension of scalars to \(\mathbb R\) gives precisely the real space \(U_\mu=\ker(B-\mu I)\cap W\), with the same dimension. Because \(A\) commutes with \(B\) and preserves \(W\), its restriction to \(U_{\mu,K}\) is a matrix over \(K\), annihilated by

\[
q_\mu(T)=T^2+T-7-\mu.
\]

For \(\mu\ne-1\), the discriminant \(29+4\mu\) is a nonsquare in \(K\), so \(q_\mu\) is irreducible. A finite-dimensional \(K\)-linear operator annihilated by an irreducible quadratic makes its underlying space a vector space over \(K[T]/(q_\mu)\). Hence its characteristic polynomial is \(q_\mu^e\) for some nonnegative integer \(e\), and

\[
\dim U_\mu=2e,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(A|_{U_\mu})=-e=-\tfrac12\dim U_\mu.
\]

The same conclusion could be phrased using rational canonical form. It is important to use the field \(K\), not only a pairing of real roots.

## 4. The eigenvalue 2 of B and the exceptional eigenvalue -1

The constant vector has \(A\)-eigenvalue 8 and \(B\)-eigenvalue 2. It is removed before applying the preceding argument. Any other \(B\)-eigenvectors with eigenvalue 2 lie in \(U_2\subset W\), and must be included in the nonsquare case: here

\[
q_2(T)=T^2+T-9,\qquad \operatorname{disc}(q_2)=37.
\]

There is no exemption for the remaining \(B=2\) eigenspace. If \(B\) has \(c\) components, its dimension is \(c-1\), necessarily even under the above quadratic argument.

For \(\mu=-1\), the equation becomes \(T^2+T-6=(T-2)(T+3)\). Let \(p,q\) be the respective multiplicities of \(A\)-eigenvalues 2 and -3 on this space. These are ordinary nonnegative integers. Every other \(B\)-eigenspace in \(W\) contributes minus half its dimension to the trace. Since \(B\) is real symmetric, its eigenspaces give a direct sum of \(W\). Therefore

\[
0=\operatorname{tr}(A)=8-\frac{62-p-q}{2}+2p-3q
 =-23+\frac52(p-q).
\]

This would imply \(46=5(p-q)\), an impossibility.

## 5. Inspection of the finite certificate generator

Inspected file: `computations/dd82/construction/n63_cyclotomic_norms.py`.

* It obtains \(\Phi_m\) by exact monic division of \(t^m-1\) by all previously computed \(\Phi_d\) for proper divisors \(d\mid m\), checking all remainders vanish.
* It uses \(F_0=2,F_1=X,F_j=XF_{j-1}-F_{j-2}\), so \(F_j(t+t^{-1})=t^j+t^{-j}\), to extract \(C_m\) from reciprocal \(\Phi_m\). It independently checks the exact lifted polynomial identity using binomial coefficients.
* The expression `sum(c[j] * 29**j * (-4)**(d-j))` has the correct sign and equals \((-4)^dC_m(-29/4)\).
* All arithmetic is integral. For every nonexceptional row it asserts strict bounding between consecutive integer squares and also supplies a modulus where the displayed norm residue is not a square. The latter yields short local witnesses without depending on floating-point approximations.
* The conductor loop includes 1 and 2 explicitly. The unique special branch is conductor 3 and asserts both its exact polynomial and norm.

The generated JSON contains the polynomials and numerical witnesses, while the Markdown table lists the small-modulus witnesses. No regeneration of the 63 norms was needed for this independent review. This review checks the reduction and implementation by inspection; it does not claim an additional independently implemented arithmetic checker or a formal kernel verification.
