### 2. Adversarial Review of `D0_EDGE_BIN_SATURATED_ROW_EXCLUSION.md`

#### A. Detailed Mathematical Derivation
The paper considers an actual order-62 graph $G$ satisfying degree $\le 8$ and diameter $\le 2$, with root $0$ realizing `D0_EDGE_BIN`:
1. **Neighborhood and Saturated Rows**:
   - $N(0) = X = \{1, \dots, 8\}$ is an independent set. Exceptional vertices $w, a \in Y$: $P(w) = \{1, 2, 3\}$, $P(a) = \{4, 5\}$, and $w \sim a$.
   - Saturated root row: $B_0 = 2e_w + e_a$.
   - In `D0_EDGE_BIN`, the third repeat $b$ of $w$ lies in $P(a) = \{4, 5\}$, canonically $b = 4$. Because $b \in P(a)$, $a \sim b$.
   - Saturated row $w$: $B_w = 2e_0 + e_b$.
   - For $i = 1 \in P(w)$, $P(w)$ gives $B_{12} = B_{13} = 1$. Row sum 3 forces a unique third unit endpoint $c \in Y \setminus \{w\}$, giving:
     $$B_1 = e_2 + e_3 + e_c.$$
2. **Commutation Entries**:
   - **Entry $(1, w)$**:
     $$(AB)_{1w} = A_{10} B_{0w} + A_{1w} B_{ww} + \sum_{y \in C_1} A_{1y} B_{yw} = 1 \times 2 + 0 + 0 = 2.$$
     $$(BA)_{1w} = B_{12} A_{2w} + B_{13} A_{3w} + B_{1c} A_{cw} = 1 + 1 + A_{cw} = 2 + A_{cw}.$$
     Commutation $AB = BA$ forces $A_{cw} = 0$ ($c \not\sim w$).
   - **Entry $(0, c)$**:
     $$(AB)_{0c} \ge A_{01} B_{1c} = 1.$$
     $$(BA)_{0c} = B_{0w} A_{wc} + B_{0a} A_{ac} = 2 A_{wc} + A_{ac} = 0 + A_{ac} = A_{ac}.$$
     Thus $A_{ac} \ge 1 \implies A_{ac} = 1$ ($a \sim c$, so $c \ne a$).
   - **Entry $(c, w)$**:
     Since $c \in Y$, $A_{c0} = 0$.
     $$(AB)_{cw} = 2 A_{c0} + A_{cb} B_{wb} = A_{cb}.$$
     $$(BA)_{cw} \ge B_{c1} A_{1w} = 1 \times 1 = 1.$$
     Thus $A_{cb} \ge 1 \implies A_{cb} = 1$ ($b \sim c$).
3. **Triangle Formation and Decisive Contradiction**:
   - The three adjacencies $a \sim b$, $a \sim c$, and $b \sim c$ form an actual triangle $\{a, b, c\}$ in $G$.
   - The edge $bc$ lies in a triangle with common neighbor $a$, forcing:
     $$c(b, c) \ge 1 \implies B_{bc} = c(b, c) + A_{bc} - 1 = c(b, c) \ge 1.$$
   - Re-evaluating entry $(0, c)$ of $AB$:
     $$(AB)_{0c} = \sum_{x \in X} A_{0x} B_{xc} \ge A_{01} B_{1c} + A_{0b} B_{bc}.$$
     Because $1, b \in X$ are distinct ($1 \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, $b \in \{4, 5\}$), both $A_{01} = 1$ and $A_{0b} = 1$.
     Therefore:
     $$(AB)_{0c} \ge 1 \times 1 + 1 \times 1 = 2.$$
   - On the other hand, $(BA)_{0c} = A_{ac} = 1$.
   - This gives $2 \le (AB)_{0c} = (BA)_{0c} = 1$, an immediate contradiction ($2 \le 1$).

#### B. Adversarial Assessment
- **Endpoints and Neighborhoods**: $c \ne 0$ because $B_{10} = 0$; $c \ne w$ because $B_{w1} = 0$; $c \notin X$ because common neighbors within $X$ are completely accounted for.
- **Scope**: The contradiction applies strictly to `D0_EDGE_BIN`. It does not apply to `D0_EDGE_BOUT`, where $b \notin P(a)$ and $a \not\sim b$.
- **Verdict**: **Airtight and fully accepted**. `D0_EDGE_BIN` is rigorously excluded.

### 3. Adversarial Review of `D0_DOUBLE_COMPONENTS_AND_D1_OUTLET.md`

#### A. Statement 1: `D0_MATCH` Forces a Closed $B_4$ Component
- **Triangle-Free Property of $w$**: $B_0 = 2e_w + e_a$, so $B_{w0} = 2$. Since $0 \not\sim w$, this double repeat is a nonedge. The remaining repeat budget at $w$ is $3 - 2 = 1$. The incident triangle budget $\sum_{v \sim w} B_{wv} = 2t(w) \le 1$ forces $t(w) = 0$. Hence $w$ is triangle-free and has no adjacent repeat neighbors.
- **Repeat Entries of $w$ into $X$**: The 5 neighbors of $w$ in $Y$ each have a single parent in $X \setminus S = \{4, \dots, 8\}$. Thus $c(w, x) = 1$ and $B_{wx} = 0$ for all $x \in X$.
- **Third Repeat $b$ of $w$**: Must lie in $Y$, so $B_w = 2e_0 + e_b$ with $b \in Y$.
- **Exclusion of $b = a$**: If $b = a$, $(AB)_{ia} = B_{0a} + B_{wa} = 2$, but $(BA)_{ia} = A_{c_i a} \le 1$, contradiction. Thus $b \ne a$ and $B_{wa} = 0$.
- **Adjacencies to $c_i$**:
  - $(AB)_{ia} \ge B_{0a} = 1 \implies (BA)_{ia} = A_{c_i a} \ge 1 \implies a \sim c_i$.
  - $(AB)_{c_i w} = A_{c_i b}$, while $(BA)_{c_i w} \ge B_{c_i i} A_{iw} = 1 \implies b \sim c_i$.
- **Distinctness of $c_1, c_2, c_3$**: $(BA)_{c_i w} \ge \sum_{h \in S} B_{c_i h} \le 1$. Since $B_{c_i i} = 1$, $B_{c_i j} = 0$ for $j \ne i$. If $c_i = c_j$, the sum would be $\ge 2$, contradiction. Thus $c_1, c_2, c_3$ are pairwise distinct.
- **Saturation of $a$ and $b$**:
  - $a$ and $b$ share common neighbors $c_1, c_2, c_3 \implies c(a, b) \ge 3$.
  - $B_{ab} = c(a, b) + A_{ab} - 1 \ge 2 + A_{ab}$.
  - Since $B_{a0} = 1$ and the row sum is 3, $B_{ab} \le 2$. This forces $A_{ab} = 0$ and $B_{ab} = 2$.
  - Rows saturate: $B_a = e_0 + 2e_b$, $B_b = 2e_a + e_w$.
  - Together with $B_0 = 2e_w + e_a$ and $B_w = 2e_0 + e_b$, this forms the closed alternating 4-cycle:
    $$0 \xrightarrow{2} w \xrightarrow{1} b \xrightarrow{2} a \xrightarrow{1} 0.$$
  - All four vertices have their row sums completely exhausted (sum = 3).
  - Graph edges in $G$ among $\{0, w, b, a\}$: $0$ is nonadjacent to $w, a, b$; $w \not\sim a$ (by template); $w \not\sim b$ ($w$ triangle-free); $a \not\sim b$ ($A_{ab} = 0$). Thus $\{0, w, b, a\}$ induces an independent set of size 4 in $G$.

#### B. Statement 2: `D0_EDGE_BOUT` Forces the Same Closed $B_4$ Component
- Here $w \sim a$, and third repeat $b \in X \setminus (S \cup \{4, 5\}) = \{6, 7, 8\}$. Thus $0 \sim b$.
- Commutation identities identical to BIN show:
  - $c_i \not\sim w$, $a \sim c_i$, $b \sim c_i$.
  - Because $b \in X$ and $c_i \in Y$, $b$ is the unique parent of $c_i$.
  - The distinctness of $c_1, c_2, c_3$ follows because $\sum_{x \in X} B_{x, c_i} = 1$.
- Since $b \notin P(a) = \{4, 5\}$, $a \not\sim b$.
- $c(a, b) \ge 3$ and $A_{ab} = 0 \implies B_{ab} = 2$.
- Rows saturate identically to:
  $$0 \xrightarrow{2} w \xrightarrow{1} b \xrightarrow{2} a \xrightarrow{1} 0.$$
- In $G$, exactly the two matching edges $0b$ and $wa$ are present; all other 4 pairs are nonedges.

#### C. Statement 3: Global Parity Forces a `D1_MATCH` Root
1. **Four-Cycle Parity Identity**:
   - Total unordered repeat weight is 93. Under triple-repeat elimination, all $B_{xy} \le 2$.
   - Let $d$ count unordered pairs with $B_{xy} = 2$. There are $93 - 2d$ unit repeat edges.
   - For an edge $xy \in E(G)$, $t(x) \le 1 \implies c(x, y) \le 1 \implies B_{xy} = c(x, y) \le 1$. Thus no edge in $G$ has $B_{xy} = 2$.
   - Exactly $3T$ unit edges are original triangle edges ($c(x, y) = 1 \implies \binom{c}{2} = 0$).
   - The remaining $93 - 2d - 3T$ unit edges are nonedges ($c(x, y) = 2 \implies \binom{c}{2} = 1$).
   - The $d$ double edges are all nonedges ($c(x, y) = 3 \implies \binom{c}{2} = 3$).
   - Double counting opposite pairs in 4-cycles gives:
     $$2 C_4 = \sum_{\{x, y\}} \binom{c(x, y)}{2} = 1 \times (93 - 2d - 3T) + 3 \times d = 93 + d - 3T.$$
2. **Parity of $d$**:
   - $2 C_4$ is even. By the triangle pairing theorem, $T$ is even, so $-3T$ is even.
   - Thus $93 + d$ is even, which forces **$d$ to be odd**.
3. **Contradiction if No `D1_MATCH` Root Exists**:
   - Suppose no `D1_MATCH` root exists.
   - Pick an arbitrary double edge $\{u, v\}$ ($B_{uv} = 2$). Take $u$ as root.
   - The recovered root-template reduction classifies every double root into D0, D1, or D2.
   - D2 is excluded; D1 is absent by hypothesis; in D0, BIN is excluded.
   - Hence $\{u, v\}$ must lie in a `D0_MATCH` or `D0_EDGE_BOUT` template.
   - By Statements 1 and 2, every such template forms a closed component containing exactly two double edges ($\{0, w\}$ and $\{a, b\}$), with no other $B$-edges leaving the 4 vertices.
   - Thus, all double edges in the graph are partitioned into disjoint pairs!
   - This forces the total count $d$ of double edges to be **even**.
   - Contradiction: $d$ cannot be both even and odd!
   - **Conclusion**: Every surviving order-62 graph must possess at least one `D1_MATCH` root.
4. **Dependencies & Open Boundaries**:
   - Explicit dependency: recovered triple-repeat elimination finite check.
   - `D1_MATCH` itself is not excluded here and remains open.
