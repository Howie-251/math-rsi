### 2. Adversarial Verification: Triangle Pairing and Simple Repeat Exclusion

#### A. Independence from Global Simplicity of $B$
The triangle pairing theorem in [`TRIANGLE_PAIRING_SIMPLE_REPEAT_EXCLUSION.md`](file:///inspire/hdd/global_user/mahaitong-240108120090/ar4math/proofs/dd82/n62/TRIANGLE_PAIRING_SIMPLE_REPEAT_EXCLUSION.md) applies to **any** legal graph $G$ on 62 vertices with maximum degree at most 8 and diameter at most 2:
1. **Regularity and Degree**: A vertex of degree $\le 7$ can reach at most $1 + 7 + 7 \times 7 = 57 < 62$ vertices in two steps. Hence $G$ is strictly 8-regular.
2. **Defect Properties**: The exact defect matrix $B = A^2 + A - 7I - J$ is symmetric, nonnegative integral, has zero diagonal, row sum 3, and commutes with $A$ ($AB = BA$).
3. **Triangle Separation**: The identity $\sum_{y \sim x} B_{xy} = 2t(x) \le 3$ implies $t(x) \le 1$ for every vertex $x$. Thus, all triangles in $G$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint.

#### B. Root-Parent Derivation and Distinct Repeat Mates
For any triangle $T_0 = \{r, a, b\}$:
- The two incident triangle edges contribute $B_{ra} = B_{rb} = 1$, exhausting 2 of the row-sum budget of 3.
- Thus each $v \in T_0$ has a unique additional repeat neighbor $f(v)$ with $B_{v, f(v)} = 1$.
- Because the triangle edges already use the full incident-edge repeat budget ($2t(v) = 2$), $f(v)$ is not adjacent to $v$.
- **Distinctness**:
  - Could $f(r) = f(a) = w$? If so, $w$ has defect weight 1 to both $r$ and $a$. If $w$ were nonadjacent to both $r$ and $a$, then $(AB)_{rw} \ge A_{ra} B_{aw} = 1$, whereas $(BA)_{rw} = B_{ra} A_{aw} + B_{rb} A_{bw} + B_{rw} A_{ww} = 0$ since $A_{aw} = A_{bw} = 0$, yielding $(AB)_{rw} > (BA)_{rw}$.
  - Could $w$ have an original edge into $\{a, b\}$? That is precisely the local `S_TOUCH` configuration. The local proof in [`S_TOUCH_SATURATED_ROW_EXCLUSION.md`](file:///inspire/hdd/global_user/mahaitong-240108120090/ar4math/proofs/dd82/n62/S_TOUCH_SATURATED_ROW_EXCLUSION.md) relies **strictly** on the local row-sum-3 budget and nonnegativity to saturate rows $r, a, b$; it never uses the assumption that all entries of $B$ elsewhere are $\le 1$.
- Consequently, $f(r), f(a), f(b)$ are pairwise distinct, nonadjacent to their respective roots, and have no original edges into $T_0$.

#### C. Vector Identities and Commutation
Let $F = \{f(r), f(a), f(b)\}$ and let $C = \bigcup_{v \in T_0} (N_G(v) \setminus T_0)$ (the 18 cross-neighbors of $T_0$).
The characteristic vector actions are:
$$B \mathbf{1}_{T_0} = 2\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + \mathbf{1}_F, \qquad A \mathbf{1}_{T_0} = 2\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + \mathbf{1}_C.$$
Commuting $A$ and $B$:
$$A(B \mathbf{1}_{T_0}) = A(2\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + \mathbf{1}_F) = 4\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + 2\mathbf{1}_C + A \mathbf{1}_F,$$
$$B(A \mathbf{1}_{T_0}) = B(2\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + \mathbf{1}_C) = 4\mathbf{1}_{T_0} + 2\mathbf{1}_F + B \mathbf{1}_C.$$
Equating both sides gives:
$$B \mathbf{1}_C = 2\mathbf{1}_C - 2\mathbf{1}_F + A \mathbf{1}_F.$$

#### D. Forcing $F$ to be an Actual Triangle
Because no vertex of $F$ has an original edge to $T_0$, $F \cap C = \emptyset$.
For any $u \in F$, $(\mathbf{1}_C)_u = 0$, which simplifies the $u$-th coordinate of the relation to:
$$(B \mathbf{1}_C)_u = -2(\mathbf{1}_F)_u + (A \mathbf{1}_F)_u = -2 + |N_G(u) \cap F|.$$
Since $B \ge 0$ and $\mathbf{1}_C \ge 0$, $(B \mathbf{1}_C)_u = \sum_{y \in C} B_{uy} \ge 0$.
Therefore:
$$|N_G(u) \cap F| \ge 2.$$
In a simple graph of order 3, $|N_G(u) \cap F| \le 2$. Thus $|N_G(u) \cap F| = 2$ for all $u \in F$.
**$F$ is forced to be a genuine triangle in $G$.**

#### E. Parity Contradiction for Simple $B$
- The map $T_0 \mapsto F$ is a fixed-point-free involution on the set of triangles of $G$, proving that **the total triangle count $T$ is unconditionally even**.
- If $B$ is simple ($B \in \{0, 1\}^{62 \times 62}$), the 93 unordered repeat edges consist of $3T$ original edges ($c(x,y)=1 \implies \binom{c(x,y)}{2}=0$) and $93 - 3T$ nonedges ($c(x,y)=2 \implies \binom{c(x,y)}{2}=1$).
- Double counting 4-cycles yields:
  $$2 C_4 = \sum_{\{x,y\}} \binom{c(x,y)}{2} = 93 - 3T.$$
  This forces $3T \equiv 93 \equiv 1 \pmod 2 \implies T$ is odd.
- **Contradiction**: $T$ cannot be both even and odd. This unconditionally excludes both simple-repeat templates (`S_TOUCH` and `S_DISJOINT`).

   - The defect of $x$ is entirely supported on $C_2 \cup C_3$ (weight 2 on $C_2$, weight 1 on $C_3$).
4. **The Isolated Point $d \in C_3$ and Contradiction**:

   - Since $A_{ac} = 0$, $d \ne c$.
   - By symmetric argument with label 2: $N_G(d) \cap C_2 = \emptyset$.

     $$(AB)_{yd} \ge A_{yx} B_{xd} = 1.$$
   - However, row $y$ has defect support strictly in $C_1 \cup C_3$, and $d$ has no neighbors in $C_1$, $C_2$, or $C_3$ (as $C_3$ is an independent set).
   - Thus:
