### 2. Adversarial Review of the D1 Structural Reduction Papers

#### A. C4 Transport and Support Gap (`D1_MATCH_C4_TRANSPORT_REFINEMENT.md`)
1. **Row 4 Decomposition**:
   - At $(4, a)$, $(AB)_{4a} = B_{0a} + B_{aa} + B_{C_4, a} = 1 + 0 + 1 = 2$.
   - Commutation forces $(BA)_{4a} = 1 + \sum_{y \ne 1} B_{4y} A_{ya} = 2$, so row 4 has exactly one unit endpoint $f \in U$ (the ordinary neighbors of $a$).
   - The third endpoint $g$ satisfies $\sum_{i \in X} B_{iy} = 2A_{wy} + A_{ay}$, forcing $g \in W$.
   - Row 4 saturates: $B_4 = e_1 + e_f + e_g$, with $f \in U$ and $g \in W$.
   - Commutation at $(2, 4)$ gives $B_{4, C_2} = A_{b4} = [b = W_4]$, which forces $f = u_2 \iff b = W_4$.
2. **The $C_3/C_4$ Defect Gap**:
   - Because $f \ne c$, $B_{4c} = 0$. Since $a$ is the unique common neighbor of $4$ and $c$, $N(c) \cap C_4 = \emptyset$.
   - From transport equation (1), $B_{C_3, y} = A_{cy} = 0$ for all $y \in C_4$.
   - Nonnegativity forces the complete block to vanish:
     $$B(C_3, C_4) = 0.$$
   - In particular, $B_{pq} = 0$.
3. **Internal Edge in $C_4$ and Light-Mate Triangle-Freeness in $B$**:
   - Summing equation (5) over $C_4$ yields entry sum 2, establishing that $B[C_4]$ consists of exactly one unordered unit edge $x_1 x_2$.
   - Saturated row $B_a = e_0 + e_p + e_q$ has $B_{0p} = B_{0q} = 0$ and $B_{pq} = 0$. Thus $a$ belongs to no unit triangle in $B$.
   - By symmetry under rerooting at $w$, the light mate $r$ likewise belongs to no unit triangle in $B$.

#### B. Internal Edges Have Triangle-Free Endpoints (`D1_MATCH_INTERNAL_EDGE_TRIANGLE_FREE.md`)
1. **Endpoints of $v_1 v_2 \in B[C_3]$**:
   - If an endpoint is $p$, $B_{pa} = 1$ and $a$ is triangle-free in $G$, so $p$ and its partner $v_j$ are triangle-free by triangle-pairing preservation.
   - If neither endpoint is $p$, their remaining repeat neighbors are distinct points $z_1, z_2 \in H = C_1$ and $y_1, y_2 \in C_2$.
   - If $v_1$ were in an original triangle, $v_1, v_2$ would be external mates in a paired prism, forcing the triangle partners $\{y_1, z_1\}$ and $\{y_2, z_2\}$ to be matched.
   - Because $B[C_2] = B[H] = 0$, the matching must pair $y_1 \leftrightarrow z_2$ and $z_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$.
   - But this gives $y_1$ two distinct repeat neighbors $z_1, z_2 \in H$, contradicting the fact that $y_1 \in C_2$ has at most one unit of repeat weight into $H$.
   - Hence $v_1, v_2$ are strictly triangle-free in $G$.
2. **Endpoints of $x_1 x_2 \in B[C_4]$**:
   - An identical argument demonstrates that an original triangle would force an external mate to have two distinct repeat neighbors in $H$, which is impossible because $H$ has only deficient matchings into $C_2, C_3, C_4$.
   - Hence $x_1, x_2$ are strictly triangle-free in $G$.

### 3. Adversarial Review of `GLOBAL_DOUBLE_TRIANGLE_QUOTIENT.md`

#### A. Verification of Key Mathematical Claims
1. **Disjointness of Generated Triangles $S_e$**:
   - For a double edge $e = \{u, v\}$, $S_e = N_G(u) \cap N_G(v)$ satisfies $|S_e| = 3$. Each pair in $S_e$ shares common neighbors $u, v$, so $B[S_e] = J_3 - I_3$ is a unit triangle in $B$.
   - Sharing 1 vertex requires repeat degree $\ge 4$ (impossible, row sum is 3).
   - Sharing 2 vertices requires 4 common neighbors in $G$ (impossible, $c(x, y) = 2$).
   - Hence generated triangles $S_e$ are pairwise disjoint, and disjoint from $D$ and original triangle vertices $T$.
   - Equation (1) holds: $|D| = 2d, |S| = 3d$, and $5d + 3T \le 62$.
2. **Companion Prism of D0 Components**:
   - In D0, $K = S_{0w} \cup S_{ab}$ forms a closed 6-vertex component in $B$ with matrix $B[K] = \begin{pmatrix} J_3 - I_3 & I_3 \\ I_3 & J_3 - I_3 \end{pmatrix}$.
   - $G[K]$ is regular of degree 0 in `D0_MATCH` (commutation forces $P = P^T$, impossible for fixed-point-free involution on 3 vertices) and degree 0 in `D0_EDGE_BOUT`.
   - The four-vertex component $C_0$ has degree 0 in `D0_MATCH` and degree 1 in `D0_EDGE_BOUT`.
3. **Equitable Component Quotient and Spectrum of $R$**:
   - The eigenspace $E_3(B)$ is precisely the span of component indicators $\{\mathbf{1}_{C_1}, \dots, \mathbf{1}_{C_k}\}$.
   - Because $AB = BA$, $A \mathbf{1}_{C_j}$ is constant on each $C_i$, proving that the partition into $B$-components is **equitable** with quotient matrix $R = (r_{ij})$.
   - The global relation $(A^2 + A - 7I - J - B) \mathbf{1}_{C_j} = 0$ reduces on the quotient to:
     $$R^2 + R = 10I + \mathbf{1} n^T.$$
   - On the codimension-1 subspace $n^T x = 0$, eigenvalues of $R$ satisfy $\lambda^2 + \lambda - 10 = 0$, with roots $\frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{41}}{2}$.
   - Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$ forces equal multiplicities $m$.
   - Hence $k = 2m + 1$ (odd), and $\text{tr}(R) = 8 - m = \frac{17 - k}{2}$, yielding the exact equation (5):
     $$\boxed{k + 2\sum_{i=1}^k r_{ii} = 17}.$$
4. **D1 Quotient Bounds**:
   - For heavy pair $0, w$ in component $H$ and $S_{0w} = \{1, 2, 3\} \subseteq K$:
     - If $H \ne K$: $0 \in H$ is adjacent to $1, 2, 3, 4 \in K \implies r_{HK} \ge 4$; and $1 \in K$ is adjacent to $0, w, a \in H \implies r_{KH} \ge 3$.
     - If $H = K$: $0$ is adjacent to $1, 2, 3, 4 \in H \implies r_{HH} \ge 4$.
5. **Port Bound (Equation 9)**:
   - With $W = V \setminus (D \cup S \cup T_{\text{orig}})$, $|W| = 62 - 5d - 3T$.
   - D1 light mates cannot lie in $D$, $S$, or $T_{\text{orig}}$, so they must lie in $W$.
   - Because each vertex in $W$ has repeat degree 3, $2d_1 \le 3|W| = 3(62 - 5d - 3T)$, yielding:
     $$\boxed{15d + 9T + 2d_1 \le 186}.$$
6. **Signed $-3$ Eigenspace**:
   - $E_{-3}(B)$ has dimension equal to the number $\ell$ of bipartite components of $B$.
   - In the signed-indicator basis, $A|_{E_{-3}(B)}$ satisfies $A^2 + A = 4I$.
   - Irreducibility of $x^2 + x - 4$ forces $\ell$ to be even and $\text{tr}(A|_{E_{-3}(B)}) = -\ell/2$.
   - Summing internal balance $s_C - t_C$ gives $\sum_{C \text{ bipartite}} (s_C - t_C) = -\ell/2$.

#### B. Audit for Forbidden Leaps or Hidden Assumptions
- **Does $A$ preserve each component?** No. The paper does not assume $A \mathbf{1}_{C_i} = c \mathbf{1}_{C_i}$; it uses the equitable partition property that $A \mathbf{1}_{C_j} = \sum_i r_{ij} \mathbf{1}_{C_i}$.
- **Do generated triangles classify whole components?** No. Section 7 explicitly states: *"components containing a generated unit triangle have not been classified, and the same component may contain both heavy edges and generated triangles."*
- **Does the D0/prism count force an even D1 count?** No. The paper explicitly warns: *"The component quotient is strongly constrained, but (4)--(9) do not force an even number of D1 double edges... No parity conclusion may be inferred solely from the at-most-two incidence bounds."*

**Verdict**: The mathematics in `GLOBAL_DOUBLE_TRIANGLE_QUOTIENT.md` is sound, rigorous, and explicitly bounded.
