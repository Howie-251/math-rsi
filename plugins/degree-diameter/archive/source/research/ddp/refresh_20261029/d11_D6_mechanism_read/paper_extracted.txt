New lower bounds for the degree-diameter problem
from generalized quadrangles and hexagons*

Abstract
We give two constructions of large graphs of small diameter from generalized polygons.
The first, for diameter three, removes from the symplectic quadrangle W (q) a point, the
points collinear with it and the lines through it, and adds Cayley edges on the orbits of the
transvections with that centre. We prove that these fibre extensions have diameter three
whenever a single graph on q2 vertices does, and that when it does not, a matching on the
points can repair the pairs it leaves at distance four. The second attaches new vertices to
incidence graphs of generalized quadrangles and hexagons: recursively, intowers of hexagons,
and to the compound graphs of G´ omez and Fiol after deleting some matching edges at a
set of pairwise non-collinear points or pairwise non-concurrent lines, which we show keeps
the diameter. The constructions improve the known lower bounds for the degree-diameter
problem at the 14 pairs (14 , 3), (15, 3), (19, 3), (20, 3), (13, 4), (14, 4), (15, 4), (17, 4), (18, 4),
(19, 4), (11, 6), (13, 6), (15, 6) and (16 , 6).

1 Introduction
The degree-diameter problem asks for N (d, k), the largest order of a graph of maximum degree
at most d and diameter at most k; see the survey of Miller and ˇSir´ aˇ n [16]. The Moore bound
M (d, k) = 1 +d Pk−1
i=0 (d − 1)i is attained only in a few cases [12, 1], and the largest known orders
are tabulated by Comellas [3] and on the Combinatorics Wiki [4].
Generalized polygons lie behind many entries of these tables. The incidence graph of a
generalized n-gon of order ( q, q) is a bipartite Moore graph of diameter n [7, 21]. Its quotients
by polarities give graphs of diameter three and five [5], and compound graphs, made of several
copies of such an incidence graph joined by perfect matchings, give many of the largest known
graphs of diameter four and six [8, 2]. Vertices have been added to such graphs before, by

1.1 Generalized polygons and a parity fact
A generalized n-gon of order (q, q) is a point-line incidence structure whose incidence graph is
bipartite and (q + 1)-regular, with diameter n and girth 2n; it has (qn − 1)/(q − 1) points and as
many lines. We use the symplectic quadrangle W (q), with n = 4, and the split Cayley hexagon
H(q) of Tits [22], with n = 6, both of which exist for every prime power q [17, 23]. Conversely,
a (q + 1)-regular bipartite graph of diameter n on 2(qn − 1)/(q − 1) vertices attains the bipartite

[21]; our verification program uses this to recognize these graphs. Two points are collinear if
they lie on a common line, and two lines are concurrent if they share a point.
Lemma 1. Let G be a connected bipartite graph with parts A and B and diameter at most k,
where k is even. Then every vertex of A is within distance k − 1 of every vertex of B [9, § 1].
Hence a new vertex joined to one vertex of A and one vertex of B is within distance k of every
vertex of G.
Proof. A path between A and B has odd length, so the distance between a vertex of A and a
vertex of B is odd and at most k, hence at most k − 1. The new vertex reaches A through its
neighbour in B, and B through its neighbour in A.

2.1 Repairing a gadget with a matching
A point p ∈ Plies on exactly one line of each class: its q + 1 lines meet ∞⊥ in q + 1 points, and
two of them on one line Mi would form a triangle with p. Write ℓi(p) for the line of Fi through
p.
Lemma 3. Let X ∪ X ′ = F∗
q, let Γ = Γ(q; X, X′; K), and let N be a matching on P such that
every pair of lines at distance more than 3 in Γ is {ℓi(p), ℓi(p′)} for some class i and some edge
pp′ of N . Then Γ with the edges of N added has diameter at most 3, and its points have degree
q + 1 + |X| or q + 2 + |X|.
Proof. Adding edges does not increase distances. In the proof of Theorem 1 only the last case
used an edge of K other than a pencil edge, so every pair at distance more than 3 in Γ consists
of two lines of one class in different pencils. Such a pair is {ℓi(p), ℓi(p′)} with pp′ ∈ N , and
ℓi(p), p, p′, ℓi(p′) is a path.
An edge pp′ of N serves one pair in each of the q + 1 classes at once, so a matching can
repair a gadget that is far from closing. For q = 9, with F9 = F3[x]/(x2 + 1), X ′ = {±1, ±x},
so that each pencil carries K3 □ K3, and X = {±1 ± x}, a 2-regular gadget gives lines of degree
9 + 4 + 2 = 15 and points of degree 10 + 4 = 14. Our gadget is a voltage lift: nine arcs ( s, s′, δ)
with δ ∈ Z9 join (u, s) to (u + δ, s′) for every pencil index u ∈ {0, . . . ,8}, the sum taken modulo
9. It leaves 72 pairs of lines at distance four in each class, 720 in all. A matching of 363 edges,
found with a constraint solver, satisfies Lemma 3 and leaves 3 points unmatched. The result
has order 1 ,539 and maximum degree 15. The gadget and the matching are part of the data.

Lemma 4. The compound C(I; T, H) has diameter at most n. More precisely, a perfect match-
ing between half-blocks Ui and Vj, with U, V∈ {P, L} and i ̸= j, puts every vertex of Ui within

Proof. From u ∈ Ui, cross to its partner in Vj, which is within distance n − 1 of every vertex

Theorem 3. Let s ≥ 0, and let C′ be obtained from C(I; T, H) by cutting every type-preserving
matching of points at one set SP of pairwise non-collinear points, and every type-preserving
matching of lines at one set SL of pairwise non-concurrent lines (Lemma 5). Add t new vertices,
each joined to one point and one line of every block, so that every vertex of C′ receives at most
s more new neighbours than the number of matching edges it lost, and join the new vertices by a
graph Y of maximum degree at most q − m + s and diameter at most n. The result has maximum
degree at most d = q + m + s and diameter at most n.
Proof. A vertex of C′ has degree at most q + m + s by construction, and a new vertex has degree
at most 2 m + (q − m + s) = q + m + s. Two vertices of C′ are within distance n by Lemmas 4
and 5. A new vertex has a point and a line of every block Ij as neighbours, so by Lemma 1 it
is within distance n of every vertex of Ij. Two new vertices are within distance n in Y .
The half-block Pi lies in deg H (i) = indeg T (i) type-preserving matchings, and Li lies in
outdegT (i) of them. So the cuts free indeg T (i) |SP | slots on Pi and outdeg T (i) |SL| slots on
Li, and the spare degree adds s slots at every vertex. For s = 0 the number t of new vertices
is at most the least of these numbers. With m = 4 and in-degrees 1 , 1, 2, 2 this gives t ≤
min(|SP |, |SL|), and with m = 5 and a regular tournament it gives t ≤ 2 min(|SP |, |SL|). When
