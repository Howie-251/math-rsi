Maximising line subgraphs of diameter at most t∗
Stijn Cambie † Wouter Cames van Batenburg ‡ R´ emi de Joannis de Verclos†
Ross J. Kang †
December 13, 2021
Abstract
We wish to bring attention to a natural but slightly hidden problem, po sed by Erd˝ os
and Neˇ setˇ ril in the late 1980s, an edge version of the degree–diameter problem. Our main
result is that, for any graph of maximum degree ∆ with more than 1 .5∆ t edges, its line
graph must have diameter larger than t. In the case where the graph contains no cycle
of length 2 t + 1, we can improve the bound on the number of edges to one that is e xact
for t ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}. In the case ∆ = 3 and t = 3, we obtain an exact bound. Our results
also have implications for the related problem of bounding the distanc e-t chromatic index,
t > 2; in particular, for this we obtain an upper bound of 1 .941∆ t for graphs of large
enough maximum degree ∆, markedly improving upon earlier bounds fo r this parameter.
Keywords: degree–diameter problem, strong cliques, distance edge-colour ing
1 Introduction
Erd˝ os in [9] wrote about a problem he proposed with Neˇ setˇ ril:
“One could perhaps try to determine the smallest integer ht(∆) so that every G
of ht(∆) edges each vertex of which has degree ≤ ∆ contains two edges so that
the shortest path joining these edges has length ≥ t . . . This problem seems to be
interesting only if there is a nice expression for ht(∆).”
Equivalently, ht(∆) − 1 is the largest number of edges inducing a graph of maximum de gree
∆ whose line graph has diameter at most t. Alternatively, one could consider this an edge
version of the (old, well-studied, and exceptionally diﬃcu lt) degree–diameter problem, cf. [3].
It is easy to see that ht(∆) is at most 2∆ t always, but one might imagine it to be smaller.
For instance, the t = 1 case is easy and h1(∆) = ∆ + 1. For t = 2, it was independently
proposed by Erd˝ os and Neˇ setˇ ril [9] and Bermond, Bond, Paoli and Peyrat [2] that h2(∆) ≤
5∆ 2/4 + 1, there being equality for even ∆. This was conﬁrmed by Chu ng, Gy´ arf´ as, Tuza
and Trotter [7]. For the case t = 3, we suggest the following as a “nice expression”.

2 A bound on ω (L(G)t) for C2t+1-free G
In this section, we prove the following theorem.
Theorem 10. Let t ≥ 2 be an integer. Let G be a C2t+1-free graph with maximum degree ∆ .
Then ω(L(G)t) ≤ |E(Tt,∆ )|. When t ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6} equality can occur for inﬁnitely many ∆ .
Since |E(Tt,∆ )| ≤ ∆ t, the expression is at most the bound desired for Conjecture 4 , and thus
this implies Theorem 7. In fact, the expression matches the o rder of the point–line incidence
graphs of generalised polygons when t ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6}, which are the examples for which equality
holds. On the other hand, by subdividing one edge of any these constructions, one can see
in the cases t ∈ { 2, 3, 4, 6} that the result fails if we omit the condition of C2t+1-freeness.
We note that Theorem 10 is a generalisation of a result in [5] w hich was speciﬁc to the case
t = 2. It is also a stronger form of a result announced in [17] for bipartite graphs. One
might wonder about excluding other cycle lengths, particul arly even ones. Implicitly this
was already studied in [14], in that the local sparsity estim ations there imply the following
statement: for any t ≥ 2 and even ℓ ≥ 2t, ω(L(G)t) = o(∆ t) for any Cℓ-free graph of maximum
degree ∆. Similarly, it would be natural to pursue a similar b ound as in Theorem 10 but for
an excluded odd cycle length (greater than 2 t + 1), which was done for t = 2 in [5].

Proof of Theorem 2. Let G = ( V, E) be a graph of maximum degree 3 such that the line

proof proceeds through a series of claims that establish str uctural properties of G.
In each claim, we will estimate |E| by performing a breadth-ﬁrst search rooted at some
speciﬁed edge e up to distance 3. To avoid repetition, let us set out the notat ion we use each
time. We write e = uv. Let u0 and u1 be the two neighbours of u other than v (if u has
degree 3). For i ∈ { 0, 1}, let ui0 and ui1 be the two neighbours of ui other than u (if ui has
degree 3). For i, j ∈ {0, 1}, let uij0 and uij1 be the two neighbours of uij other than ui (if uij
has degree 3). Similarly, deﬁne vi, vij, vijk for i, j, k ∈ {0, 1}.
Claim 15. G contains no triangle, loop or multi-edge.
Proof. These 3 cases are straightforwardly bounded by the breadth- ﬁrst search. If the edge
