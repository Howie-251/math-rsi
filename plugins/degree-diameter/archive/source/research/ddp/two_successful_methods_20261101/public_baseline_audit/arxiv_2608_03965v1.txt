August 24, 2026 Abstract For positive integers d d and k k , let n k ​ ( d ) n_{k}(d) be the maximum order of a graph of maximum degree at most d d and diameter at most k k . We prove that lim d → ∞ n k ​ ( d ) d k = 1 \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}(d)}{d^{k}}=1 for every fixed k k , thereby resolving the asymptotic degree–diameter problem for fixed diameter and proving a conjecture of Bollobás. At most how many vertices can a graph G G have if its maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) is at most d d and its diameter diam ⁡ G \diam G is at most k k ? In this work we obtain an asymptotically tight answer to that question. Define n k ​ ( d ) := max ⁡ { | V ⁡ ( G ) | : Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ d ​ and ​ diam ⁡ G ≤ k } . n_{k}(d):=\max\{|V(G)|:\Delta(G)\leq d\text{ and }\diam G\leq k\}. (1) A breadth-first search from any vertex gives the Moore bound n k ​ ( d ) ≤ 1 + d ​ ∑ j = 0 k − 1 ( d − 1 ) j = d k + O k ​ ( d k − 1 ) . n_{k}(d)\leq 1+d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(d-1)^{j}=d^{k}+O_{k}(d^{k-1}). (2) A graph attaining equality in the Moore bound is called a Moore graph .
Complete graphs attain the bound when k = 1 k=1 , and the odd cycle C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} attains it when d = 2 d=2 . Apart from these families and the trivial degree-one cases, the Petersen graph and the Hoffman–Singleton graph attain the bound for ( d , k ) = ( 3 , 2 ) (d,k)=(3,2) and ( 7 , 2 ) (7,2) , respectively. The only remaining potential nontrivial Moore graph would have degree 57 57 and diameter 2 2 ; no Moore graph exists for any other parameters [ 19 , 4 , 14 ] . We refer to the survey of Miller and Širáň [ 21 ] for further background on the degree–diameter problem. The rarity of exact equality leads naturally to the asymptotic problem in which k k is fixed and d d tends to infinity. Bollobás conjectured that, for every fixed k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there are infinitely many d d for which n k ​ ( d ) ≥ ( 1 − ε ) ​ d k ; n_{k}(d)\geq(1-\varepsilon)d^{k}; equivalently, lim sup d → ∞ n k ​ ( d ) d k = 1 \limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}(d)}{d^{k}}=1 ; see the discussion in [ 8 , p. 2] . This longstanding conjecture was previously known only for k ∈ { 2 , 3 , 5 } k\in\{2,3,5\} , through constructions arising from finite generalized polygons [ 16 , 17 , 3 ] . For all sufficiently large k k , Canale and Gómez proved that infinitely many degrees Δ \Delta admit a graph of diameter k k and order greater than ( 0.629 ​ Δ ) k (0.629\,\Delta)^{k} [ 11 ] ; see also [ 8 , Proposition 5] . This was the strongest coefficient known uniformly over all sufficiently large diameters.
Another variant of Bollobás’ conjecture [ 6 , 7 ] is that lim inf d → ∞ n k ​ ( d ) d k = 1 \liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}(d)}{d^{k}}=1 . We prove that stronger statement. Theorem 1.1 . For every fixed positive integer k k , lim d → ∞ n k ​ ( d ) d k = 1 . \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}(d)}{d^{k}}=1. (3) Our construction uses complete flags in 𝔽 q 2 ​ k + 1 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{2k+1} . The vertices are the even-rank subflags, and two vertices are adjacent when some odd-rank subflag extends both to complete flags.
Any two complete flags are induced by two orderings of a common basis. Odd-even transposition sorting (alternating rounds of disjoint adjacent swaps) transforms one ordering into the other, and since only every second round changes the even ranks, the corresponding vertices are joined by a path of length at most k k . Each vertex has exactly ( q + 1 ) k (q+1)^{k} extensions to a complete flag, yielding a natural upper bound on the degree of the graph. The Moore bound shows that this upper bound is asymptotically tight, and the prime number theorem supplies suitable field sizes for all sufficiently large degree bounds. We also consider the edge version of the problem, posed by Erdős and Nešetřil [ 18 ] and studied recently by Cambie, Cames van Batenburg, de Joannis de Verclos, and Kang [ 8 ] .
