H3++

Degree = 4, Diameter = 6; Order = 760; Moore bound=1457;

Obtained by Nishant Bhakar (nishant.bhakar@gmail.com, Redmond, WA, USA; October 15, 2026), who set the research, selected the topics to pursue, and reviewed the results. Claude Fable assisted with the computational searches, code development, analysis, and summarization of the results.
The graph is obtained from the incidence graph of the generalized hexagon H(3), which has 364 points, 364 lines, degree 4, girth 12 and diameter 6. A set of 32 point-line incidences (flags) is selected, and each corresponding edge is subdivided by inserting a new vertex. These 32 vertices are then joined by a 2-regular graph consisting of three cycles of lengths 8, 8 and 16, preserving degree 4 and producing a graph of order 760. The selected flags form two orbits of length 16 under a Sylow 2-subgroup of order 128 contained in the centraliser of an involution of Aut(H(3)) = G2(3):2. The additional edges connect vertices only within the same orbit and were determined using exact-cost simulated annealing. The vertices are labelled 0?363 for points, 364?727 for lines and 728?759 for the inserted vertices.

H4+

Degree= 5, Diameter = 6; Order =2836; Moore bound=6826;

Obtained by Nishant Bhakar (nishant.bhakar@gmail.com, Redmond, WA, USA; October 15, 2026), who set the research, selected the topics to pursue, and reviewed the results. Claude Fable assisted with the computational searches, code development, analysis, and summarization of the results.
The graph is constructed from the incidence graph of the generalized hexagon H(4), which has 1365 points, 1365 lines, degree 5, girth 12 and diameter 6. A set of 106 point-line incidences (flags) is selected, and each corresponding edge is subdivided by inserting a new vertex. These 106 vertices are then joined according to a cubic graph F, preserving degree 5 and producing a graph of order 2836. The graph F, of diameter 6 and with 881 vertex pairs at distance 6, was first obtained using simulated annealing with edge switches to minimise the number of pairs at distance 6 or more. With F fixed, the 106 flags were then selected using a min-conflicts relocation algorithm. The vertices are labelled 0?1364 for points, 1365?2729 for lines and 2730?2835 for the inserted vertices.

New vertices of one spread line form a clique; each new vertex has 3 further "external" edges. Obtained by Francesc Comellas (September 24, 2026), with the help of OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol. From graph H7(K5), consider one of the four K5 copies introduced. Three vertices have degree seven and two have degree eight. Introduce a new vertex and join it to all five vertices of K5. Delete one of the external edges of the two vertices that now have degree nine and join the vertices of degree seven to the new vertex added. The new vertex has now degree seven. Perform this modification independently at each of the four exceptional K5's, thus four new vertices have been introduced, each with degree seven. Finally, add two independent edges between these new vertices. The final graph is 8-regular and has 39676 and diameter 6. Now consider again one copy of K5 and choose one external edge incident with each of its five vertices. Delete these five edges and introduce a new vertex and join it to all the five vertices of K5, thus all its vertices keep degree eight. The new vertex is joined now to three of the five vertices that have degree seven. By testing all different choices one results in a graph that has 39677 vertices, maximum degree 8, and diameter 6, although it is no longer regular. A similar process, adding a new vertex, repairs 8-regularity and leads to a 39678 graph with size 158712, avg.dist: 5.34846 and girth: 3.
