# Why the two anchored domains cover the fixed family

Let six shifts g_ij be arbitrary nonzero residues modulo 20, with g_ji=-g_ij. Edges join `(i,z)` to `(j,z)` and `(j,z+g_ij)`. The complete NB3 predicate is invariant under the following graph and port isomorphisms.

1. A permutation of the four base vertices sends each zero matching to a zero matching and each shifted matching to a shifted matching. Indeed, the three K4 one-factors are the three partitions of its four vertices into two pairs, so every base permutation permutes the one-factors. It induces the same permutation on the three zero ports and the three shifted ports. This preserves consecutive-port inequalities. When an unordered base edge reverses its canonical lower-to-higher orientation, its written shift changes sign.
2. For any unit u modulo20, `(i,z)->(i,uz)` multiplies all written shifts by u and preserves zero edges, shifted edges, ports, and nonbacktracking words. The possible shifts remain nonzero.

If any shift is a unit, a base permutation moves its edge to01, leaving some unit h as its signed shift. Deck multiplication by h^-1 then gives g01=1. The other five shifts remain arbitrary in {1,...,19}, exactly the original exhaustive domain. This is a reduction of the unit-bearing family, not an assertion that every nonzero residue is a unit.

For connectivity, let H be the subgroup of Z20 generated by the six shifts. Every edge changes the deck coordinate by zero or plus/minus one shift, so every walk stays in a fixed coset of H. Conversely, every deck has a complete zero K4, allowing movement between all base vertices without changing its deck. For i<j the shifted edge `(i,z)->(j,z+g_ij)` followed by the zero edge `(j,z+g_ij)->(i,z+g_ij)` realizes the displacement g_ij while returning to the same base. Reversing this walk realizes -g_ij. Starting at any base, zero edges allow use of each such displacement and return to the starting base. Thus a component consists exactly of all four bases over one coset of H. In particular, the controller is connected if and only if

`gcd(20,g01,g02,g03,g12,g13,g23)=1`.

A disconnected controller has vertices in different components. Such vertices are nonadjacent and have no walk of any length, hence fail the required NB3 predicate. Disconnected cases can therefore be excluded without enumerating their words.

The nonzero nonunits are U={2,4,5,6,8,10,12,14,15,16,18}. A connected all-nonunit assignment must contain an odd residue, since all even shifts generate a subgroup of the even residues. Its odd nonunit is 5 or15. Move that edge to01; the resulting signed value is still 5 or15. If it is5, multiply decks by1; if15, multiply decks by3, since 3*15=5 mod20. Both multipliers are units and permute U. This gives g01=5 and all other shifts in U.

In this normalized domain, the common gcd already divides gcd(20,5)=5. It is5 precisely when all five other shifts are divisible by5, namely all lie in {5,10,15}; otherwise it is1. Exactly 243 of the 161,051 assignments are disconnected, leaving 160,808 connected assignments. This establishes the complete reduction without assuming an inverse for any nonunit.

The normalized unit and nonunit domains are disjoint, because unit multiplication and sign preserve whether each residue is a unit. Together with the disconnected-case argument, they cover the entire fixed six-nonzero-shift K4 / Z20 family. The proof only concerns this matching-labeled controller construction and its specified full NB3 source interface.
