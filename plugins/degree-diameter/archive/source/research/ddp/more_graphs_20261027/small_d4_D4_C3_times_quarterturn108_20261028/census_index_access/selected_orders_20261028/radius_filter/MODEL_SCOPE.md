# Published census filter: exact model and acceptance scope

The inputs are the selected69 members of the authors' complete connected simple quartic Cayley census: orders42 through53 for diameter3, and105 through161 for diameter4. We wait for the downloader's COMPLETE_DOWNLOAD receipt; this task makes no network requests. The author census completeness claim is inherited, not re-proved.

A sparse6 line is decoded into integer adjacency bitsets. Its order must equal the filename order, every edge must be a distinct nonloop undirected edge, every vertex must have degree4, and there must be2n edges. These checks bind the actual literal graph rather than only the archive member name. The first examples of the small and extended sparse6 order encodings are also compared once with installed NetworkX's parser, without a repeated graph-distance computation.

For each decoded graph, one exact root0 radius3 or radius4 check is enough for a negative answer. A root0 pass is not recorded as a new complete graph: the producer checks all its roots before exporting it, and the parent then independently accepts a first improvement. Complete radius coverage also implies connectivity. No negative global diameter is inferred from a radius failure.

The degree4 Moore bounds are53 at diameter3 and161 at diameter4. Degrees at most3 have corresponding bounds22 and46, below the improvement intervals. Therefore the selected published quartic census is sufficient for improved Cayley graphs of maximum degree at most4 in these two target cells. This conclusion remains explicitly conditional on the published census completeness and the full download/decoding receipt. It does not classify all graphs, establish a new census, or decide the open non-Cayley cases.

The two earlier explicit order108 module-action experiments are not rerun. Order108 in the published catalogue is processed only as one member of the newly authorized complete-census filter, with its own source and accounting. There is no new generator search, solver, group audit, hash, or graph-family expansion.
