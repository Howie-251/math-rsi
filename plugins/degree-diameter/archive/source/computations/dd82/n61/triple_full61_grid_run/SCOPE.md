## Why these simultaneous labels cover the original graph domain

The new proof first shows that each of the four F points has a different U neighbor: every U point must reach w in two steps through F, and the four F points each have exactly one U neighbor. With the old F labels retained, including r=Z[0],s=Z[1] in Case I, label that unique neighbor of F[j] as U[j]. Label its actual matched V partner as V[j]. This preserves the U/V identity matching and does not prescribe any V-to-F permutation.

The proof next shows that every pair `(Ta[i],U[j])` has a common neighbor in Z outside F. There are20 such pairs and exactly20 points outside F, each with one Ta and one U neighbor. Every such cell therefore has one point. Give it the previously free label `Z[4+4*i+j]`. The labels of Ta,Tb,Tc,Td, their original three normalized Ta matchings, and F were not changed by this step. These labels consequently fix all of the following blocks at once:

```text
A[U[j],F[k]] = [j=k]
A[U[j],Z[4+4*i+k]] = [j=k]
A[Ta[i],Z[4+4*h+j]] = [i=h]
A[Ta[i],F[k]] = 0
```

The last relation was already forced by the original layer-degree constraints and is now also an explicit unit. The two free Tb-to-Tc/Td permutations, both Tc/Td cases, the γδ edge alternatives, all96 V-Z entries, remaining unfixed T-Z entries, and permitted internal Z edges keep their original variables. In particular the analogous Ta-by-V incidence fact is not turned into a second incompatible coordinate fixation. Neither C nor σ is selected.

Every actual connected triple target has the added labels, so a future certified UNSAT result still addresses the original connected triple branch, subject to the inherited reductions and independent source review.
