                                                   Abstract. The n-dimensional hypercube graph Qn has as vertices all subsets of {1, . . . , n},
                                                   and an edge between any two sets that differ in a single element. The Ruskey-Savage con-
                                                   jecture states that every matching of the n-dimensional hypercube Qn can be extended into
                                                   a Hamilton cycle. We prove that matchings of Qn containing edges spanning at most d = 5
                                                   directions can be extended into a Hamilton cycle. We also characterize when these match-
                                                   ings of most d = 5 directions can be extended into a Hamilton path between two prescribed
                                                   vertices. Our proofs work for arbitrary d and n where d ≤ n assuming some extension
                                                   properties hold in Qd which we verified by a computer for d = 5.

                                                   Keywords: Hypercube · Hamilton cycle · Hamilton path · Matchings spanning limited in
                                                   number of directions


                                           1     Introduction

                                           The n-dimensional hypercube Qn is the graph whose vertices are all subsets of [n] := {1, . . . , n} and
                                           whose edges connect sets that differ in a single element. There is a large literature on structural
                                           properties of this class of graphs which comes from research on the topological structure and
                                           analysis of hypercubic interconnection networks [24]. For example, Gros in 1872 [15] proved that Qn
                                           contains a Hamilton cycle for every n ≥ 2. A Hamilton path in the hypercube Qn is in the literature
                                           also called a Binary Gray Code which is a listing of all binary strings of length n such that every
                                           two consecutive strings differ in exactly one positions. It is named after Frank Gray who in 1953
                                           constructed a special Hamilton cycle in Qn , called Reflected Binary Gray Code, and use it in his
                                           U.S. Patent 2632058 for signal processing. Then, Gray codes have found applications in such diverse
                                           areas as information retrieval, image processing or data compression, and alternative constructions
                                           of Gray codes satisfying certain additional properties have been widely studied [16,20].
                                              1.1 Extending matching to Hamilton cycle. Ruskey and Savage [19] asked the following
                                           question which is still open.

                                           Conjecture 1 (Ruskey and Savage [19]). Every matching of the hypercube Qn can be extended
                                           into a Hamilton cycle where n ≥ 2.

                                               One natural step toward this problem is considering perfect matchings only, where each vertex
                                           is incident to exactly one edge. Kreweras [17] conjectured that every perfect matching in the n-
                                           dimensional hypercube with n ≥ 2 extends to a Hamilton cycle. This conjecture was popularized
                                           by Knuth [16] and proven by Fink [10]. The proof of Kreweras’ conjecture actually provides the
                                           following stronger statement where K(Qn ) denote the complete graph on vertices of Qn .

                                           Theorem 1 (Fink [10]). For n ≥ 2 and every perfect matching M of K(Qn ), there exists a per-
                                           fect matching N of Qn , such that M ∪ N is a Hamilton cycle of K(Qn ).

   This result inspired several generalizations [1,13], e.g. the authors of [1] showed that Kreweras’
conjecture also holds for sparse spanning regular subgraphs of hypercubes. Using the following
theorem, Alahmadi et al. [2] proved that for every perfect matching M in Qn the set of edges e
of Qn such that M ∪ {e} cannot be extended into a Hamilton cycle forms a matching of Qn .

Theorem 2 (Alahmadi et al. [2], Theorem 4). Let n ≥ 2 be an integer and let M be a perfect
matching on Qn . Let e1 , e2 ∈ E(Qn ) \ M incident with the same vertex. Then Qn has a perfect
matching N such that N contains one of e1 , e2 and M ∪ N is a Hamilton cycle of Qn .

    Shujia Wang and Fan Wang [23] presented another relaxation of the Ruskey and Savage con-
jecture. For vertices u and v connected by an edge uv, we call the position i ∈ [n] where they differ
the direction of that edge, with both endpoints u and v being incident to and covered by this
edge. A matching M in Qn spans in direction i if it contains at least one edge in that direction,
and it spans at most d directions if its edges lie in at most d distinct directions.

Theorem 3 (Shujia Wang, Fan Wang [23]). For n ≥ 5, let M be a matching on the hyper-
cube Qn , such that |M | < 10 · 2n−5 and M spans at most 5 directions. Then M can be extended
to a Hamilton cycle.

   We expand upon their idea, considering only matchings spanning at most 5 dimensions, and
prove that the size restriction on M is unnecessary.

Theorem 4. For n ≥ 2, let M be a matching on the hypercube Qn that spans at most 5 directions.
Then M can be extended to a Hamilton cycle.

   Our proof of Theorem 4 works for arbitrary many directions d assuming the following conjecture
holds. Each vertex has a parity determined by the parity of number of ones in its binary string
representation.

Conjecture 2. Given a matching M on the hypercube Qn and two vertices u, v ∈ V (Qn ) of opposite
parity, where u is not covered by M , there exists a Hamilton path extending M on Qn and
connecting u and v where n ≥ 2.

    Observation 14 proves that Conjecture 2 implies Conjecture 1. We verify this conjecture for
2 ≤ d ≤ 5 in Observation 16 using a computer program1 which provides us Theorem 4. For general
d, we have the following conditional statement.

Theorem 5. For n ≥ d ≥ 2, let M be a matching on the hypercube Qn that spans at most d
directions. If Conjecture 2 holds for dimension d, then M can be extended to a Hamilton cycle.

    1.2 Extending matching to Hamilton path. It is well-known that the hypercube Qn
contains a Hamilton path between any two prescribed end vertices of opposite parity [21]. Gregor,
Novotný, and Škrekovski [14] considered the problem of extending a perfect matching of Qn to
a Hamilton path between two prescribed end vertices with opposite parity. Their proof works in
the more general setting of the complete bipartite graph B(Qn ) which contains an edge between
all pairs of vertices of Qn with opposite parities. For a matching M of Qn and one of its vertices u,
we write uM for the other end vertex of the edge of M incident with u. A layer in direction i ∈ [n]
consists of all edges in direction i, and a half-layer is a subset of a layer containing only edges of
specific parity.

Theorem 6 (Gregor, Novotný, and Škrekovski [14], Theorem 2). Let n ≥ 5, and let u, v
be two vertices of opposite parity in Qn . A perfect matching M of B(Qn ) with uv ∈
                                                                                  / M can be
extended to a Hamilton path, by edges from Qn , with end vertices u and v if and only if and
(M \ {uuM , vv M }) ∪ {uM v M } contains no half-layers.

   Motivated by this result, we ask when a matching of Qn can be extended into a Hamilton path
between prescribed vertices; and we conjecture the following statement. We call a set of edges
an almost half-layer if it is missing one edge to become a half-layer. For a vertex u, we denote
by ui its neighbor in direction i. An almost half-layer is u-avoiding if it lacks only one edge incident
to u to form a complete half-layer.

Conjecture 3. Let n ≥ 5 and u, v ∈ V (Qn ) be vertices of opposite parity. For a matching M on Qn ,
there exists a Hamilton path between u and v extending M if and only if none of these conditions
holds:

C1. M contains a half-layer and u and v are covered by it,
C2. M contains a u-avoiding almost half-layer in the direction i where v = ui , j ̸= i and
    uuj , vv j ∈ M ,
C3. uv ∈ M .

   Observation 10 proves that all C-conditions are necessary for the existence of a Hamilton path,
and our main conjecture is that they are also sufficient. Note that the requirement n ≥ 5 is
necessary, since counterexamples for n = 4 presented in [14] applies here as well, with one extra
counterexample shown √  in Figure 1. Furthermore, Dvořák and Fink [6] constructed a matching
of B(Qn ) of size Θ(2n / n) which cannot be extended into a Hamilton path for n ≥ 9. Using a
similar construction, Observation 15 presents a matching of B(Qn ) which cannot be extended to
a Hamilton cycle between any two vertices for n ≥ 9, so we restrict Conjecture 3 to consider only
matchings of Qn . Observation 13 proves that Conjecture 3 implies 2.

    1.3 Related results on extending edges to cycles. The research on Hamilton cycles
in hypercubes satisfying certain additional properties has received considerable attention; see e.g.
the survey by Savage [20]. They are motivated by applications in computer networks, where the hy-
percube is frequently used as a network topology with a number of desirable properties, such as
small degree and diameter.
    Vandenbussche and West [22] who conjectured that every matching can be extended into
a 2-factor, and the conjecture was proven by Fink [11]. Fink and Mütze [12] proved that every
matching of Qn can be extended into a cycle that visits at least a 2/3-fraction of all vertices.
    Dvořák [9] showed that any set of at most 2n − 3 edges of Qn , n ≥ 2, that induces vertex-
disjoint paths is contained in a Hamilton cycle. Dvořák and Gregor [7] proved that for any set E
of at most 2d − 4 edges in Qn , n ≥ 5, that form disjoint paths and two vertices x and y of opposite
parity that are neither internal vertices of the paths nor end vertices of the same path, there is
a Hamilton path with end vertices x and y that contains all of E.
    Dimitrov et al. [5] proved that the hypercube Qn contains a Hamilton cycle avoiding a given
matching except a forbidden configuration.
    Other related results are nicely surveyed in the introduction of recently published paper [12].
    1.4 Overview of our approach. In this paper, we consider matchings M in Qn spanning
at most d ≤ n directions. So, it is natural to split Qn into 2n−d subcubes of dimension d so
that every edge of M belongs into one of these subcubes. We find a Hamilton cycle extending M
by finding cycles extending M in every subcube so that these cycles can be interconnected into
a single Hamilton cycle. A similar approach was presented by Gregor [13] who proved that every
perfect matching N of Qn can be extended into a Hamiltonian cycle by adding only edges I inside
prescribed subcubes if and only if N ∪ I is connected. The connectivity condition is clearly neces-
sary, so in our case where M ⊆ I, we have to use some edges between subcubes in the Hamilton
cycle extending M , but our approach use only few of them.
    Extending matchings into a cycle and a path is presented in Section 2 and 3, resp. In Appendix
A we show some important relations between conjectures that were discussed in introduction.
Programs verifying our statements for d = 5 is described in Appendix B.


2      Extending to a Hamilton cycle

In this section, we show how to extend a matching M on the hypercube Qn that spans at most d
directions into a Hamilton cycle. First, we prove Theorem 5 giving the extension for n ≥ d ≥ 2,
but it assumes that Conjecture 2 holds for dimension d. Then, using Observation 16 which verifies
Conjecture 2 for d = 5, we prove Theorem 4.

    First we introduce some notation. The Cartesian product G□H of graphs G and H has vertex
set V (G) × V (H), with edges between vertices (u, v) and (u′ , v ′ ) if either u = u′ and vv ′ ∈ E(H),
or v = v ′ and uu′ ∈ E(G). Note that Qn = Qn−d □Qd . We call the copies of H in G canonical
copies. In this paper, we consider matchings on Qn that span at most d directions, and we can
assume without loss of generality that these directions are the last d directions. Therefore, every
edge uv of these matchings belongs to one canonical copy of Qd in Qn , and this canonical copy
is uniquely identified by the first n − d directions u which are the same as in v. For a vertex
u ∈ V (Qn ), we denote by uL and uR its first (Left) n−d and last (Right) d coordinates, respectively.
Let Qn [uL ] be subgraph of Qn induced by vertices w such that wL = uL . In another works, Qn [uL ]
is the canonical copy of Qn containing u. Similarly for a matching M spanning only the last d
directions, M [uL ] are edges of M in Qn [uL ].

3    Extending to Hamilton path

The extension of matchings to Hamilton paths presents additional challenges beyond the cycle case.
Half-layers create rigid structures that constrain possible paths through the hypercube, making
6

them natural obstacles to path extension. This phenomenon was observed by Gregor, Novotný
and Škrekovski [14] in their study of path extensions in bipartite graphs derived from hypercubes.
   Their work, presented in Theorem 6, characterizes path extensions from perfect matchings in
B(Qn ). They also showed an equivalent result. For a graph G, let G−X denote the graph obtained
by removing vertices in set X and all incident edges.

Theorem 9 (Gregor, Novotný, and Škrekovski [14], Theorem 3). Let u, v be vertices of
opposite parity in Qn , n ≥ 5, and let M be a perfect matching of B(Qn − {u, v}). There exists
a matching N ⊆ E(Qn ) such that N ∪ M forms a Hamilton cycle of B(Qn − {u, v}) if and only
if M does not contain a half-layer.

   The dimension requirement n ≥ 5 proves essential, providing sufficient flexibility for routing
Hamilton cycles through the hypercube while accommodating matching constraints. Smaller di-
mensions lack this flexibility, as demonstrated by n = 4 where certain matching configurations
provably [14] prevent Hamilton path extensions.
   This characterization builds on a fundamental structural property of half-layers:

Lemma 1 (Gregor, Novotný, and Škrekovski [14], Lemma 4). Let Hi and Hj be half-layers
of Qn of different directions i and j, respectively. Then, every edge of Hi is incident with some
edge of Hj .

   This intersection property has important implications: if a matching contains a half-layer in
some direction i, it cannot contain even a single edge of a half-layer in any other direction j, as
these edges would necessarily share endpoints. We can see that if a perfect matching contains
two half-layers, they both must be of the same direction and of opposite parity. We also need
the following lemma regarding almost half-layers:

Lemma 2 (Gregor, Novotný, and Škrekovski [14], Lemma 6). Let M be a matching of
K(Qn ), n ≥ 4. Then, M contains almost half-layer in at most one direction.

    The pathological nature of half-layers is most evident when considering the C-conditions:

Proof. Negation of condition C3 is trivially necessary. We prove only the necessity of case C2,
since the C1’s proof is similar. Both cases are illustrated in Figure 3.
    For C2, let i, j be as defined as in the condition, with H denoting the u-avoiding almost half-
layer in direction i. Without loss of generality, assume u is even, and v is odd. Let A be the set of
even vertices, except u, covered by H and B the set of odd vertices not covered by H, except v.
For path to exist between u and v extending M , each vertex in B must have an edge incident
to it, that is not in the direction of i. That holds also for uj , since the edge uj v j would close
the path prematurely. So each vertex in B needs its counterpart from A. However, |B| = 2n−2
while |A| = 2n−2 − 1, making such a path construction impossible.

    The conditions C1 and C2 have structural implications: when either holds, M must contain
either a half-layer or an almost half-layer in some direction i and from Lemma 1 and Lemma 2
only in that direction. Thus, only C1 or C2 may hold at once. Moreover, these conditions natu-
rally suggest how M could be completed to a perfect matching: any extension must use edges in
direction i to maintain the half-layer or the almost half-layer structure. We call this completion
the unique extension of M , as it aligns with the conditions characterized in Theorem 6. Since any
Hamilton path extending the unique extension must use all its edges, such a path also extends M .
    The following lemma characterizes when there exists a Hamilton path between a vertex and
one of its neighbors:
Lemma 3. Let d ≥ 5, u ∈ V (Qd ), and M be a matching of Qd . Assuming Conjecture 3 holds
for dimension d, then for at most two of the neighbors of u there does not exist a Hamilton path
extending M connecting them to u.
Proof. Consider which neighbors of u are forbidden by C-conditions. Under condition C1, u must
be covered by a half-layer in direction i. This forbids only neighbor ui both by C1 and C3 be-
cause uui ∈ M lies in the half-layer, while all other neighbors remain valid as they lie on the correct
side of the half-layer.
    Under condition C2 with neighbor v = ui , M contains a u-avoiding almost half-layer in direc-
tion i and edges uuj , vv j ∈ M for some j ̸= i. This forbids exactly two neighbors: v = ui by C2
and uj by C3, since uuj ∈ M .
    As C1 and C2 cannot hold simultaneously, at most two neighbors are forbidden, implying any
set of three neighbors contains at least one admitting a Hamilton path extending M .

    Beyond examining paths to neighbors, we consider paths to all vertices of opposite parity from
a given vertex u. Let TuM denote the set of vertices v where a Hamilton path exists between u
and v extending M . This notation enables us to quantify exactly how many endpoints are available
for Hamilton paths:

Observation 11. Let n ≥ 5, M be a matching of Qn , and u ∈ V (Qn ). Assuming Conjecture 3
holds for n, then |TuM | ≥ 2n−2 . Moreover, |TuM | = 2n−2 if and only if M contains a half-layer H
covering u, in which case TuM consists exactly of vertices with parity opposite to u not covered by
H. Otherwise |TuM | ≥ 2n−1 − 2.
Proof. If u is not covered by M , none of the C-conditions can hold between u and any vertex of
opposite parity. Thus |TuM | = 2n−1 . For the remainder of the proof, we assume u is covered by M
and analyze three cases.
    First, suppose M contains a half-layer H covering u. By condition C1, TuM consists exactly of
vertices of opposite parity to u that are not covered by H, giving |TuM | = 2n−2 .
    Next, suppose M contains a u-avoiding almost half-layer in direction i. Then TuM excludes
exactly two vertices of opposite parity. uM , that is forbidden by C3 since uuM ∈ M and ui , that
is forbidden by C2. Therefore |TuM | = 2n−1 − 2.
    Finally, if neither C1 nor C2 holds, only uM is forbidden by C3, yielding |TuM | = 2n−1 − 1.
    Therefore, |TuM | = 2n−2 occurs if and only if C1 holds. In this case, by Lemma 1, the direction
and parity of H are uniquely determined by the complement of TuM . In all other cases, |TuM | ≥
2n−1 − 2 for n ≥ 5.

    This observation gives us an important information about the Hamilton paths extending
matching M and stemming from a specific vertex u. Either we have many available endpoints
(|TuM | ≥ 2n−1 − 2) or M contains a specific half-layer covering u that determines the endpoint set
as all of the vertices of opposite parity to u not covered by the said half-layer.
    To analyze path constructions systematically, we introduce cube sequences as our primary
structural tool. A sequence of m cubes of dimension d is the graph Pm □Qd , where Pm is the path
on m vertices. For a path Y = (y1 , . . . , ym ) in Qn−d where m ≤ 2n−d , its induced sequence in Qn
consists of subcubes Gi = Qn [yi ] and edges between them, with corresponding matchings Mi =
M [yi ] for i ∈ [m]. We refer to this as the induced sequence of cubes and matchings of path Y .
    Any cube sequence embeds as a subgraph into Qn for sufficiently large n, allowing us to
extend our hypercube notation to these sequences. Vertices and edges in cube sequences exhibit
a dual nature: they possess both local properties within their canonical copy of Qd and global
properties from their embedding in Qn . For instance, in Qn = Qn−d □Qd , a vertex u ∈ V (Qn )
with uL = 1 ∈ V (Qn−d ) and uR = 0 ∈ V (Qd ) is locally even in its Qd copy due to uR = 0, while
its global parity in Qn is determined by uL = 1.
    A sequence of half-layers in Pm □Qd consists of half-layers in each canonical copy of Qd , all
sharing the same direction and global parity. These sequences exhibit an important alternation
property: when traversing consecutive cubes in the sequence of cubes, half-layers of the same global
parity must alternate between odd and even local parity within their respective Qd copies—a prop-
erty inherent to the embedding structure.

Lemma 4. Let d ≥ 5 and G = P2 □Qd with canonical copies G1 , G2 of Qd containing match-
ings M1 , M2 respectively. Let u ∈ G1 , v ∈ G2 have different global parities. Assuming Conjecture 3
holds for dimension d, there exists a Hamilton path between u and v extending M = M1 ∪ M2 if
and only if no sequence of half-layers in G contained in M covers both u and v. Moreover, when
such a path exists, there are at least 2d−3 distinct Hamilton paths, determined by different edges
between G1 and G2 satisfying these conditions.

Proof. We begin by proving necessity. If G contains a sequence of half-layers in M covering both u
and v, this sequence corresponds to a half-layer in Qd+1 covering u and v. By Observation 10, no
Hamilton path can exist.
    For sufficiency, assume no sequence of half-layers in M covers both u and v. Let TuM1 and TvM2
be their respective sets of available endpoints in their respective copies G1 and G2 . Given w ∈
(TuM1 )R ∩ (TvM2 )R , let w1 ∈ V (G1 ), w2 ∈ V (G2 ) such that w1R = w and w2R = w. By definition
of TuM1 and TvM2 , there exist Hamilton paths Z1 from u to w1 extending M1 in G1 and Z2 from v
to w2 extending M2 in G2 . The path Z1 ∪ {w1 w2 } ∪ Z2 forms a Hamilton path in G extending M .
    To establish the bound on the number of distinct Hamilton paths, we consider several cases.
When u or v is not covered by a half-layer in M , say u, then by Observation 11, |TuM1 | ≥ 2d−1 − 2.
Therefore |(TuM1 )R ∩ (TvM2 )R | ≥ |TvM2 | − 2 ≥ 2d−2 − 2 ≥ 2d−3 for d ≥ 5.
    When both vertices are covered by half-layers, these must differ either in direction or global
parity. In the case of same direction but different global parity, (TuM1 )R = (TvM2 )R with |TuM1 | =
2d−2 . When the directions differ, the overlap has size exactly 2d−3 , as this corresponds to fixing
two coordinates for vertices of specific parity. Thus in all cases, we obtain at least 2d−3 distinct
choices for w, each yielding a distinct Hamilton path.

a vertex a ∈ G1 let a′ ∈ G2 be its unique corresponding counterpart, such that aR = (a′ )R . For
a free edge ab, we say it admits a Hamilton path if there exists a Hamilton path in G2 extending M2
between the corresponding vertices a′ and b′ , which we denote by Za′ ′ b′ . Given such an admitting
edge ab in some path Zv , we can construct a Hamilton path in G as Zv \ {ab} ∪ {aa′ , bb′ } ∪ Za′ ′ b′ .
    We claim that there exists at least one free edge admitting a Hamilton path. Suppose toward
contradiction that no such admitting free edge exists.
    Let x be the first vertex where paths Zv1 and Zv2 diverge, with Zv1 following edge xy and Zv2
following xz. Note that neither y nor z can be an end vertex v1 or v2 , as this would force the paths
to be identical, since the only vertex left to visit is v1 = v2 .
    It is clear that both of the vertices y and z must be incident to at least three edges from these
paths, since they differ at these vertices. By Lemma 3 at most two of them can be free, since if
there would be three, at least one of them would admit a Hamilton path. Since only one incident
edge may be fixed we get two free edges and one fixed for both of these vertices.

half-layers, with w its corresponding vertex in G1 . The two other free edges coming from y and z
are exactly yw ∈ Zv2 and zw ∈ Zv1 .

    All remaining free edges must lie in direction i, since they do not admit a Hamilton path,
and thus alternate with fixed edges. This is forcing Zv1 and Zv2 to be identical except for their
behavior at aforementioned vertices. The path up to x is the same for both of the paths, but
the vertices y, z and w must be connected somehow. If there would be a path, common to both
of Zv1 and Zv2 , connecting y and w directly, without using z, there would be a cycle together with
the edge yw in Zv1 . The same applies to z and w. Thus there must be a path between y and z
without using w. However, this creates a path between vertices of the same parity, using an even
number of edges alternating between free and fixed - an impossibility since the path starts and
ends with a fixed edge.

to construct the desired path in G.

Observation 12. Conjecture 3 implies Conjecture 4.

Proof. Let M bea maximal matching. We prove the equivalence between a C-condition holding

creating M ′ preserves one of them. When C2 holds, M contains a u-avoiding almost half-layer

occur.

B    Computer verification
We establish the validity of Conjecture 2 for dimensions 2 ≤ n ≤ 5, along with Conjecture 5 for n =
5 through computer-assisted verification techniques inspired by the MathCheck methodology [4].
The source code for this section is available at https://gitlab.com/jirka.fink/matching_of_
five_directions.
    Our verification strategy centers on systematically enumerating feasible matchings of Qn and
verifying the existence of Hamilton paths between vertices of differing parity. To accomplish this,
we develop a Sat representation of the matching constraints and employ the CaDiCaL solver [3]
for matching generation. After each matching is generated and verified, we exclude both it and its
isomorphic variants from subsequent iterations, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the solution
space.

on maximal matchings. This approach is justified since any non-maximal matching extends to
a maximal configuration and inherits its path extension properties, except of the matchings sub-
ject to the C-conditions, that will be handled separately for Conjecture 5. For Conjecture 2, we
further optimize by forcing vertex 0 to remain uncovered during maximal matching generation,
as the conjecture requires at least one avoided vertex.

    The verification process systematically examines terminal vertex pairs for each maximal match-
ing. For Conjecture 2, we pair vertex 0 with each odd vertex, while for Conjecture 5, we consider
all opposite-parity vertex pairs not subject to C-conditions. This direct verification establishes
the following observation:

Observation 16. Conjecture 2 holds for 2 ≤ d ≤ 5.

    For Conjecture 5, we must also verify matchings M and terminal pairs u, v where adding
a single edge e would cause M ∪ {e} to violate C-conditions. We identify such matchings through
a two-step process: First, we start with maximal matchings that violate C-conditions and iter-
atively remove edges until reaching minimal violating configurations. Then, from each minimal
configuration, we remove one edge at a time to obtain matchings that are exactly one edge away
from violating C-conditions. For these matchings, we verify the existence of two distinct Hamil-
ton paths between each valid terminal pair. This approach completes our proof for n = 5, as
any matching not violating C-conditions either extends to a C-condition-free maximal matching
or extends to a matching that is one edge away from violating C-conditions. These verifications
establish:

Observation 17. Conjecture 5 holds for d = 5.

    Initial verification attempts on an Apple MacBook M3 Max proved intractable since the gen-
eration of maximal matchings did not finish even after 3 days. We achieved significant speedup by
partitioning the search space: since maximal matchings either cover all vertices or avoid specific
non-adjacent vertex combinations, we generated these combinations up to isomorphism. There
were 159 such combinations, and we verified them in parallel.
    The optimized algorithm reduced maximal matching generation for n = 5 to 6 hours, produc-
ing 16,459 non-isomorphic and 59,457,409 isomorphic matchings, with Hamilton path verification
completing in 2 minutes. A specialized handcrafted algorithm, inspired by [12], further reduced
matching generation time to 3 minutes. The subsequent verification of the cases one edge away
from violating C-conditions takes 20 minutes. For n = 4, complete verification executes in under
10 seconds.
