# Static review of the degree-eight graph and incidence argument

Reviewed 2026-10-13 by the Codex Lean subagent. `review_independence: same-family`; `acceptance_status: provisional`. Scope: `low-math/EIGHT.md`, Steps 2–7, and the sixteen supplied residual representatives in `EIGHT_VERIFIED.json`. The complete 12,103-profile classification and its probability certificates were **not** re-executed in this review. No Lean proof of this branch is claimed.

Within that scope, the argument is coherent and I found no mathematical gap. It is a genuine general incidence proof conditional on the stated complete local alternatives, not an enumeration of global hypergraphs.

The following connections were explicitly checked:

1. A codegree-three pair forces every triple containing either endpoint and two other low points to have zero common multiplicity. If the fixed second endpoint is in that triple, choose an extra low vertex; if it is not, the desired quadruple already contains it. Since at least ten low points exist, these choices are always possible. Thus each of the eight incident edges at the first endpoint covers at most one of the other at least nine low points. This contradiction does not assume different neighbors choose distinct edges; that injectivity is the conclusion of the preceding intersection bound.
2. Once codegree three is excluded, translating the supplied residuals gives exactly the five listed graph shapes. The common four-point count is one for C4 and zero for the other four shapes. For four selected locks, this common count equals the exact four-lock count; common triple counts must instead include the exact four-lock count. The JSON consistently makes that distinction.
3. Three common unit neighbors force two C4-containing hyperedges to coincide through the actual codegree-one pair. Their common five-point edge then contains an induced claw quadruple, violating its zero common count. The fact that a hyperedge has five points alone would not suffice; the claw condition is correctly used.
4. The universal-star argument treats graph degree at least four and graph degree three separately. In the latter case an outside point sharing exactly two of three independent neighbors creates a P3+P1 on those three neighbors and itself. This justifies excluding that outside point rather than silently assuming it is adjacent to all three.
5. For the universal star, four leaves cannot lie in a hyperedge, so every edge contains at most three leaves. The incidence identities count each edge once, with multiplicities `j` and `choose(j,2)`. Equality after `choose(j,2)≤j` forces every nonempty leaf intersection to have size three. An edge through the universal center and any leaf, required by pair coverage, contradicts this equality because a claw forbids three leaves in that edge. Both total counting and local claw information are needed.
6. With maximum graph degree two, an induced P3 allows at most two outside vertices: its center has no free neighbor slot and its endpoints have one each. An outside vertex adjacent to both endpoints consumes two slots, so this only strengthens the bound. With maximum degree one and no induced 2K2, at most one graph edge remains. The final inequality `r(r−9)≤e≤1` contradicts `r≥10`.
