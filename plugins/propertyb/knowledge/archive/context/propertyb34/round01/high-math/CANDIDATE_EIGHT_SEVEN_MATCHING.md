# Eight degree-seven matching locks on 24 vertices

## Statement and status

Let H be a simple 5-uniform hypergraph with 34 edges on 24 vertices. Suppose
eight selected vertices all have degree seven and their pair codegrees equal
one except for a matching of codegree-two pairs. Then H has Property B.

The same evidence also proves the mixed variant with seven degree-seven
vertices and one degree-six vertex, provided the low vertex is unit with all
seven high vertices and the high/high nonunit pairs form a codegree-two
matching. This variant is needed when the selected eight-set contains exactly
one low vertex and that vertex is reserved as the actual eighth lock.

This is a complete conditional paper candidate. Its link-overlap quotas require
only an upper bound of 23 on the other vertices, not global pair covering.
All retained arithmetic witnesses have independent labelled-injection replay.
The conditional statement and finite classification are not yet in Lean.

## Complete seven-lock domain

Any seven selected vertices have a matching of zero through three nonunit pairs,
so at least one is isolated in that matching. Remove such an isolated vertex.
The first six selected locks have the complete pair-budget decompositions
already generated by the first-uncovered-pair recursion. Their matching sizes
zero through three have 352, 467, 611, and 787 labelled decompositions.

Append the isolated seventh point. Its incident old positive traces form a
partition of the six old labels; each part must be an existing trace of size
at most four. Promote one occurrence of each part to include the new point,
then restore new singleton degree seven and the empty count. This generates
every actual profile. Negative counts are discarded.

Exact invariant refinement followed by all remaining vertex bijections gives
1,494 seven-lock orbits. This refinement uses only isomorphism invariants; it
does not discard any bijection between vertices with identical refined data.
The generation and bounds are `SEVEN_MATCHING_QMAX0.json` and
`SEVEN_MATCHING_QMAX0_BOUNDS.json`.

1,482 orbits have direct total/pure-quota bounds below one. Of the twelve
remaining types, three have strict shared-vector rational duals, independently
checked in `SEVEN_MATCHING_QMAX0_JOINT_INDEPENDENT.json`. The remaining groups are
745, 759, 784, 817, 826, 1124, 1137, 1387, and 1393.

## Attach the actual eighth point

In each of these nine seven-lock profiles the nonunit graph is a three-edge
matching. The eighth point is either unit with all seven old points, or has
codegree two with the unique old isolated point and unit codegree with all
others. The edge-extension recursion allocates these seven pair requirements
among actual old trace capacities; it never promotes an old five-point trace.
Singleton restoration uses the actual new degree, and empty restoration
preserves exactly 34 edges.

`EIGHTH_MATCHING_Q0.json` contains a complete superset of these inputs: it also
permits a new degree-six point unit with all seven old points. There are 3,212
labelled extension entries and 882 combined orbits. Of these, 881 have direct
strict bounds. Every direct witness has independent A/B/C replay.

The unique residual (group 404) has degree seven at all eight locks and matching
pairs (0,1), (2,3), (4,5), (6,7). Its two four-point traces are 60 and 195, which
partition the locks. The exact repaired dual in `EIGHTH_MATCHING_Q0_JOINT.json`
uses four layouts and proves

\[
U\le\frac{5002501389}{5005000000}<1.
\]

`EIGHTH_MATCHING_Q0_JOINT_INDEPENDENT.json` independently checks each raw A/B/C
row, both ordered pair weights, every quota, capacity penalty, and the final
rational inequality. The LP only proposed the four-layout witness; the saved
exact inequality is the certificate. Thus the original random-order coloring
has a successful outcome for every actual input in the statement.
