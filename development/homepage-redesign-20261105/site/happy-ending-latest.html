Objects and the two additions

N(m,r,s) is the maximum population of a complete ordered rank-3 signotope avoiding blue r-vertex paths, red s-vertex paths and weak m: opposite-colour paths with the same actual endpoints and total length at least m+2. Path length counts vertices. Write Qₘ = N(m,m−1,m) and Fₘ = N(m,m,m). The R20 chart and reverse-extension manuscript gives Fₘ = Qₘ + 1; geometric inclusion then gives ES(m) ≤ Fₘ + 1. The junction-restricted residual Dₘ is a different capacity from ordinary N(m,m−2,m).

One input, four sources of deficit

On one actual Qₘ input X, let S and B be the long red and blue source sets, O their common complement, and b = |S ∩ B|, p = |O|. For ordinary capacity bounds u and t at the same m, keep every possible penultimate vertex Vq of the last owner of each distinct lock q. The proof constructs actual ordinary subdomains I and Y on this same X.

δ = u + t − b − p = (u − |I|) + (t − |Y|) + Σq(|Vq| − 1) + |L ∖ O| ≥ 0

The four terms charge two actual short-cap deficits, extra penultimate choices, and locks outside O. Owners may share locks; distinct locks have disjoint penultimate sets. Keeping all paths is essential. Deleting vertices requires recomputing sources, locks and deficits.

(m − 2)|X| + ρ + ε + (m − 3)δ = (m − 1) binom(2m − 7, m − 4) + (m − 3)u + (m − 2)t

Here ρ is total ordinary-row slack and ε is the one-blue-source slice deficit. Both are nonnegative. The same-input budget turns compatible path structure into population loss without combining unrelated extremal realizations.
